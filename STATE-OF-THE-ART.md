## How the pieces may fit together

**An infection → The immune system misfires → Small blood vessels work poorly → Muscles run short of energy + Brain and nerves are affected → Crash after activity (PEM)**

One leading model, from Wirth and Scheibenbogen, of how the findings may connect. Parts are well supported, others are still being tested — and not every patient may fit it. ([Wirth 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2020.102527); [Scheibenbogen 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.13669); [Watton 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08319-3))

## What ME/CFS is

**A serious, long-lasting physical illness whose defining feature is a crash after even small amounts of activity.** · _Well established_

- The hallmark is post-exertional malaise (PEM): symptoms get markedly worse after physical or mental effort, often with a delay. Modern diagnostic criteria put PEM at the centre. ([Carruthers 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2796.2011.02428.x); [National Academies Press 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK274235/))
- PEM can be measured objectively: on two exercise tests 24 hours apart, patients cannot repeat their first-day performance. ([Keller 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/1479-5876-12-104))
- Roughly 0.5–1% of people are affected worldwide, depending on the definition used. ([Lim 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-020-02269-0))
- Many cases start after an infection. After COVID-19, about one in five people in a large New York survey reported PEM, and post-COVID patients increasingly resemble established ME/CFS over time. ([Packard 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.31.26356614); [Azcue 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08321-9); [Komaroff 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molmed.2021.06.002))
- International experts have now agreed on a common way to measure the core symptoms, which should make studies easier to compare. ([Kujawski 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-026-05161-8))

## Genes: a real biological basis

**Large genetic studies show ME/CFS has a real, inherited component that points mainly to the nervous and immune systems.** · _Strong evidence_

- DecodeME, the largest genetic study so far (over 15,000 patients), found eight DNA regions linked to ME/CFS, several involved in fighting infection. ([Ponting 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.06.25333109))
- Independent groups have since replicated gene hits in other datasets, with the strongest signals in brain and nerve-signalling genes rather than blood. ([Slaughter 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.09.26362115); [Maccallini 2026](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9702020/v1))
- Genetic analyses suggest ME/CFS has its own roots in nerve cells and is not simply a form of depression, even though symptoms can overlap; in a cohort followed after infection, inherited depression risk did not predict who developed a CFS-like post-infective illness. ([Lee 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.08.26355171); [Saini 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2026.08.006); [Ruseva 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2026.10098))
- Genetic ‘cause-and-effect’ methods point to cellular energy production and low-grade inflammation and clotting as likely causes, not just consequences, of the illness. ([Wielscher 2026](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9363637/v2))

## Immune system & autoantibodies

**The immune system is clearly involved, and ‘self-attacking’ antibodies are a leading theory — but removing them has so far given mixed results.** · _Mixed / contested_

- Many patients carry autoantibodies (antibodies that mistakenly target the body’s own receptors) against receptors that control blood vessels and nerves. This is the basis of the Scheibenbogen group’s autoimmune model. ([Loebel 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2015.09.013); [Sotzny 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2018.01.009); [Azcue 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-49131-9))
- Lab evidence that such antibodies can do harm: patients’ antibodies damaged cells’ energy machinery, and long-COVID antibodies caused symptoms when transferred into mice. ([Liu 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbih.2026.101187); [Santos Guedes de Sá 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.04.042))
- The ‘first-response’ (innate) immune system tends to overreact, and a calcium channel in immune cells (TRPM3) is faulty — a finding confirmed across several labs. ([Che 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44324-025-00079-w); [Sasso 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2025.1703924); [Magawa 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/eji.70240))
- Filtering antibodies out of the blood (immunoadsorption) lowered antibody levels but did not beat a sham treatment in a German post-COVID trial; another small study was also negative. Two Berlin studies are still following it in antibody-positive patients. ([Stortz 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101744); [Wossidlo 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s15010-026-02914-8); [IMPACT](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07529197); [IA-Surv](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00033897))
- Wiping out B cells with rituximab failed in a large trial, but targeting the antibody-making plasma cells (daratumumab) helped 6 of 10 patients in a small pilot. Charité plans a placebo-controlled B-cell trial (inebilizumab). ([Fluge 2019](https://doi.org/10.7326/M18-1451); [Fluge 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2025.1607353); [PIONEER_PAIS](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07724834))

## Blood vessels & circulation

**Poorly working small blood vessels and disturbed blood flow are among the most consistent findings.** · _Strong evidence_

- The inner lining of blood vessels (endothelium) works poorly in many patients; a simple eye scan showed this was worst in the ME/CFS group of post-COVID patients. ([Haffke 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-022-03346-2); [Wallraven 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-026-05144-9))
- During exercise, blood does not return to the heart properly (‘preload failure’), and muscles struggle to use the oxygen they receive. ([Joseph 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2021.01.082); [Mackay 2026](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.90080.2026))
- Dizziness and a racing heart on standing (orthostatic intolerance, POTS) are common — over a third of teenagers with ME/CFS in one study had POTS. ([Leone 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fped.2026.1836407); [Miwa 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1744154))
- Whether low blood volume drives this is debated: Wirth proposes it does, while a new measurement study found blood volume made no difference to who was affected. ([Wirth 2026](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202602.1993.v1); [Yamazaki 2026](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.71087))
- ‘Microclots’ (tiny, hard-to-dissolve clots) that may clog small vessels are reported in long COVID and ME/CFS — a theory with growing lab evidence, but not yet tested as a treatment target in the trials tracked here. ([Nunes 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.26360874); [Grixti 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/qrd.2026.10022))

## Muscles & energy production

**Muscles and cells’ energy supply are genuinely affected — this is where the Wirth/Scheibenbogen drug MDC002 aims.** · _Emerging evidence_

- Wirth and Scheibenbogen’s theory: too little blood flow lets sodium and then calcium build up in muscle cells, damaging their energy units (mitochondria) and causing the crash after activity. ([Wirth 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2020.102527); [Wirth 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-021-02833-2); [Scheibenbogen 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.13669))
- Muscle biopsies differ from people after 60 days of bed rest — so the muscle changes are not just from being inactive. ([Charlton 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75725-y))
- Blood chemistry points to a ‘low-energy survival mode’ and a blocked step in turning food into energy; brain scans show extra lactate, a sign of energy made without enough oxygen. ([Naviaux 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1607571113); [Fluge 2016](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.89376); [Bader 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.10.26359935); [Heng 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2025.102514))
- Not everything holds up: a careful repeat of a well-known study found no ‘blood factor’ that disturbs healthy muscle cells. ([Ryback 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341334))
- MDC002 (Mitodicure, Klaus Wirth) is designed to break this calcium-overload cycle in muscle. A first trial in humans is planned but not yet registered. ([Wirth 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2025.1693589); [MDC002](https://mitodicure.com/))

## Brain & nervous system

**Brain scans and spinal-fluid tests show measurable changes, but it is still unclear which of them drive the illness.** · _Emerging evidence_

- Fatigue appears to start partly in the brain: when muscles tire, the brain’s compensating signal barely increases in ME/CFS. ([Bedard 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2026.104041))
- Brain chemistry is altered: spinal fluid shows too little activity of noradrenaline (an alertness messenger), and the stress hormone cortisol is consistently low. ([Aregawi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcag173); [Woo 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-026-03608-1); [Wirth 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27094041))
- The brain’s nightly ‘cleaning system’ seems weaker, which matches worse sleep and brain fog. ([Thapaliya 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1875420); [Sun 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag090))
- Brain inflammation is not settled: an early scan study found signs of it, while a recent post-COVID study found no immune attack inside the brain. ([Nakatomi 2014](https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.113.131045); [Erhart 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.10.26359944))

## Viruses & triggers

**Infections, and dormant viruses ‘waking up’, are a likely trigger — but antiviral treatment has not helped so far.** · _Mixed / contested_

- Common dormant viruses (like Epstein-Barr virus and HHV-6) often reactivate during severe COVID-19, and this has been linked to later long COVID. ([Maguire 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10740-z); [Chen 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745245); [Choudhury 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106455))
- Leftover pieces of coronavirus have been found in gut tissue of long-COVID patients. ([LaFranchi 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.07.743616))
- Yet the largest antiviral trial (Paxlovid) showed no benefit, and a drug against ‘fossil virus’ proteins in our DNA (temelimab) also failed. ([Baden 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00406-8); [Nehme 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2026.106892))

## Gut

**Gut problems are common and linked to overall severity; treating the gut is an early but plausible idea.** · _Early ideas_

- About one in three patients also has irritable bowel syndrome, and worse gut symptoms go along with worse ME/CFS symptoms and more inflammation. ([Rajabi 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1881784); [Brown 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08442-1))
- Patients lack some ‘good’ bacteria, including ones that make a fuel for the gut lining. ([Xiong 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chom.2023.01.001); [Donchev 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines14061183))
- A small placebo-controlled trial of a probiotic reduced fatigue in long COVID; it needs repeating in more people. ([Amoroso 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/bjbs.2026.16993))

## Tests & biomarkers

**There is still no validated lab test for ME/CFS, but several candidates look promising.** · _Emerging evidence_

- Blood tests based on DNA folding (96% accuracy in one test group) and on RNA fragments (about 77%) are the most advanced candidates; both still need wider testing. ([Hunter 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-025-07203-w); [Gardella 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2507345122))
- Large blood-protein and ‘cell-particle’ studies find consistent differences, including work from the Scheibenbogen group. ([Hoel 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102647); [Seifert 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27052314); [Eguchi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08695-w))
- Simple physical tests such as repeated hand-grip strength show real, measurable weakness and could track treatment effects, though they cannot diagnose on their own. ([Mihatsch 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08654-5); [Wunderle 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08579-z))
- Patients seem to fall into subgroups — for example, a heavier-symptom group more often after an infection — but how to define them reliably is still open. ([St-Jean 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.29.26356818); [Green 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfimm/iqag010))

## Treatment: what has been tested

**There is no approved drug for ME/CFS yet; several ideas show early promise, and several well-run trials were negative.** · _Mixed / contested_

- Early positive signals: the antibody-neutraliser BC007 in a placebo-controlled long-COVID trial, daratumumab in a small pilot, and hyperbaric oxygen in a Charité cohort (no placebo group). All need confirmation. ([Hohberger 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103358); [Fluge 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2025.1607353); [Kim 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08324-6))
- Clearly negative or disappointing: rituximab, Paxlovid, temelimab, immunoadsorption vs sham, and repurposed drugs that gave only a small, temporary benefit. ([Fluge 2019](https://doi.org/10.7326/M18-1451); [Baden 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00406-8); [Nehme 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2026.106892); [Stortz 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101744); [Wall 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00242-2))
- Exercise programmes can ease some symptoms but do not improve physical capacity, and patients rate graded exercise as least helpful. Better-organised care alone did not improve function in a Charité trial. ([Zhao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychores.2026.112677); [Eckey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2426874122); [Peters 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2026.09.013))
- Trials often miss what patients actually rely on: only about 1 in 14 registered post-COVID trials test a patient-prioritised treatment, and few measure PEM as the main outcome. ([Gabriel 2026](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10851936/v1))
