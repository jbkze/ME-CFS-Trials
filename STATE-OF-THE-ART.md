## How the pieces may fit together

**An infection → The immune system misfires → Small blood vessels work poorly → Muscles run short of energy + Brain and nerves are affected → Crash after activity (PEM)**

This is one leading model, proposed by Wirth and Scheibenbogen. Some steps are well supported, others are still being tested, and it may not fit every patient. ([Wirth 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2020.102527); [Scheibenbogen 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.13669); [Watton 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08319-3))

## What ME/CFS is

**A serious, long-lasting physical illness. Its main feature is a crash after even small amounts of activity.** · _Well established_

- The main symptom is post-exertional malaise (PEM). After physical or mental effort, symptoms get much worse, often with a delay. Current diagnostic criteria are built around it. ([Carruthers 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2796.2011.02428.x); [National Academies Press 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK274235/))
- PEM can be measured. In two exercise tests 24 hours apart, patients cannot repeat their performance from the first day. ([Keller 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/1479-5876-12-104))
- Depending on the definition, roughly 0.5 to 1% of people worldwide have ME/CFS. ([Lim 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-020-02269-0))
- Many cases start after an infection. In a large New York survey, about one in five people reported PEM after COVID-19, and post-COVID patients come to look more and more like ME/CFS over time. ([Packard 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.31.26356614); [Azcue 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08321-9); [Komaroff 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molmed.2021.06.002))
- International experts have agreed on a common way to measure the core symptoms. That makes studies easier to compare. ([Kujawski 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-026-05161-8))

## Genes

**Large genetic studies show that ME/CFS has an inherited part, mainly involving the nervous and immune systems.** · _Strong evidence_

- DecodeME is the largest genetic study so far, with more than 15,000 patients. It found eight DNA regions linked to ME/CFS, several of them involved in fighting infection. ([Ponting 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.06.25333109))
- Other groups have found the same gene hits in other data. The strongest signals are in genes for the brain and nerve signals, not in the blood. ([Slaughter 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.09.26362115); [Maccallini 2026](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9702020/v1))
- ME/CFS seems to have its own genetic roots in nerve cells. It is not a form of depression, even if some symptoms overlap. In people followed after an infection, inherited depression risk did not predict who developed a CFS-like illness. ([Lee 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.08.26355171); [Saini 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2026.08.006); [Ruseva 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2026.10098))
- Genetic cause-and-effect analyses point to cellular energy production, low-grade inflammation and clotting as likely causes of the illness, not just results of it. ([Wielscher 2026](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9363637/v2))

## Immune system & autoantibodies

**The immune system is clearly involved. Self-attacking antibodies are a leading theory, but removing them has had mixed results so far.** · _Mixed results_

- Many patients have autoantibodies against receptors that control blood vessels and nerves. These are antibodies that attack the body's own receptors. A similar pattern shows up when the illness followed an infection and, less often, a vaccination. Scheibenbogen's autoimmune model is based on this. ([Loebel 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2015.09.013); [Sotzny 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2018.01.009); [Azcue 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-49131-9); [Dinser 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.18.26363380))
- Lab studies show that these antibodies can do harm. Patients' antibodies damaged the energy machinery of cells, and antibodies from long-COVID patients caused symptoms in mice. ([Liu 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbih.2026.101187); [Santos Guedes de Sá 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.04.042))
- The first line of immune defence (innate immunity) tends to overreact. A calcium channel in immune cells called TRPM3 is faulty, and several labs have confirmed this. ([Che 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44324-025-00079-w); [Sasso 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2025.1703924); [Magawa 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/eji.70240))
- Immunoadsorption filters antibodies out of the blood. In a German post-COVID trial it lowered antibody levels but did no better than a sham procedure, and a second small study was also negative. Two studies in Berlin are still following it in antibody-positive patients. ([Stortz 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101744); [Wossidlo 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s15010-026-02914-8); [IMPACT](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07529197); [IA-Surv](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00033897))
- Removing B cells with rituximab failed in a large trial. Targeting the plasma cells that make antibodies with daratumumab helped 6 of 10 patients in a small pilot. Charité is planning a placebo-controlled B-cell trial with inebilizumab. ([Fluge 2019](https://doi.org/10.7326/M18-1451); [Fluge 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2025.1607353); [PIONEER_PAIS](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07724834))

## Blood vessels & circulation

**Small blood vessels that work poorly and disturbed blood flow are among the most consistent findings.** · _Strong evidence_

- The inner lining of the blood vessels (endothelium) works poorly in many patients. A simple eye test showed this most clearly in post-COVID patients who meet ME/CFS criteria. ([Haffke 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-022-03346-2); [Wallraven 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-026-05144-9))
- During exercise, too little blood returns to the heart (preload failure), and the muscles have trouble using the oxygen they get. ([Joseph 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2021.01.082); [Mackay 2026](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.90080.2026))
- Dizziness and a racing heart when standing (orthostatic intolerance, POTS) are common. In one study, more than a third of teenagers with ME/CFS had POTS. ([Leone 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fped.2026.1836407); [Miwa 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1744154))
- It is unclear whether low blood volume is behind this. Wirth thinks so, but a recent study that measured blood volume found it made no difference. ([Wirth 2026](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202602.1993.v1); [Yamazaki 2026](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.71087))
- Microclots are tiny clots that are hard to dissolve and may block small vessels. They have been found in long COVID and ME/CFS. Lab evidence is growing, but none of the trials listed here targets them yet. ([Nunes 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.26360874); [Grixti 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/qrd.2026.10022))

## Muscles & energy

**Muscles and the energy supply of cells are affected. This is where the Wirth and Scheibenbogen drug MDC002 comes in.** · _Some evidence_

- The theory of Wirth and Scheibenbogen: with too little blood flow, sodium and then calcium build up in muscle cells. This damages their power plants (mitochondria) and causes the crash after activity. ([Wirth 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2020.102527); [Wirth 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-021-02833-2); [Scheibenbogen 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.13669))
- Muscle samples from patients look different from those of healthy people after 60 days of bed rest, so the changes are not just from inactivity. ([Charlton 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75725-y))
- Blood tests point to a low-energy survival mode and a blocked step in turning food into energy. Brain scans show extra lactate, a sign that energy is being made without enough oxygen. ([Naviaux 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1607571113); [Fluge 2016](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.89376); [Bader 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.10.26359935); [Heng 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2025.102514))
- Not every result holds up. A careful repeat of a well-known study found no factor in patients' blood that harms healthy muscle cells. ([Ryback 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341334))
- MDC002 from Klaus Wirth's company Mitodicure is designed to break this cycle of calcium overload in muscle. A first trial in humans is planned but not registered yet. ([Wirth 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2025.1693589); [MDC002](https://mitodicure.com/))

## Brain & nervous system

**Brain scans and spinal-fluid tests show measurable changes. Which of them drive the illness is still unclear.** · _Some evidence_

- Part of the fatigue seems to start in the brain. When muscles tire, the brain normally sends a stronger signal to make up for it. In ME/CFS this signal hardly rises. ([Bedard 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2026.104041))
- Brain chemistry is changed. Spinal fluid shows too little noradrenaline activity, a messenger for alertness, and the stress hormone cortisol is low. ([Aregawi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcag173); [Woo 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-026-03608-1); [Wirth 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27094041))
- The brain's cleaning system during sleep seems weaker. That fits with poor sleep and brain fog. ([Thapaliya 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2026.1875420); [Sun 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag090))
- It is not settled whether there is inflammation in the brain. An older scan study found signs of it. A recent post-COVID study found no immune attack in the brain. ([Nakatomi 2014](https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.113.131045); [Erhart 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.10.26359944))

## Viruses & triggers

**Infections and dormant viruses that become active again are likely triggers. Antiviral treatment has not helped so far.** · _Mixed results_

- Common dormant viruses such as Epstein-Barr virus and HHV-6 often become active again during severe COVID-19. This has been linked to later long COVID. ([Maguire 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10740-z); [Chen 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.17.745245); [Choudhury 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106455))
- Pieces of the coronavirus have been found in the gut tissue of people with long COVID. ([LaFranchi 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.07.743616))
- But the largest antiviral trial, with Paxlovid, showed no benefit. A drug against proteins from old virus sequences in our DNA (temelimab) also failed. ([Baden 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00406-8); [Nehme 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2026.106892))

## Gut

**Gut problems are common and go along with more severe illness. Treating the gut is an early idea that makes sense.** · _Early ideas_

- About one in three patients also has irritable bowel syndrome. Worse gut symptoms go with worse ME/CFS symptoms and more inflammation. ([Rajabi 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1881784); [Brown 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08442-1))
- Patients lack some helpful gut bacteria, including ones that make a fuel for the gut lining. ([Xiong 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chom.2023.01.001); [Donchev 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines14061183))
- In a small placebo-controlled trial, a probiotic reduced fatigue in long COVID. It needs to be repeated with more people. ([Amoroso 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/bjbs.2026.16993))

## Tests & biomarkers

**There is still no validated lab test for ME/CFS, but several candidates look promising.** · _Some evidence_

- The most advanced candidates are blood tests based on how DNA is folded (96% accuracy in one test group) and on RNA fragments in the blood (about 77%). Both need wider testing. ([Hunter 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-025-07203-w); [Gardella 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2507345122))
- Large studies of blood proteins and of tiny particles released by cells find consistent differences. Some of this work comes from Scheibenbogen's group. ([Hoel 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102647); [Seifert 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27052314); [Eguchi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08695-w))
- Simple physical tests such as repeated grip-strength measurements show real weakness and could track the effect of treatment. On their own they cannot diagnose ME/CFS. ([Mihatsch 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08654-5); [Wunderle 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08579-z))
- Patients seem to fall into subgroups, for example a group with heavier symptoms that more often started after an infection. How to define these groups reliably is still open. ([St-Jean 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.29.26356818); [Green 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfimm/iqag010))

## Treatment: what has been tested

**There is no approved drug for ME/CFS yet. Some ideas show early promise, and several well-run trials were negative.** · _Mixed results_

- Early positive signals: BC007, which blocks autoantibodies, in a placebo-controlled long-COVID trial; daratumumab in a small pilot; and hyperbaric oxygen in a Charité study without placebo group. All of them need confirmation. ([Hohberger 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103358); [Fluge 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2025.1607353); [Kim 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08324-6))
- Negative or disappointing: rituximab, Paxlovid, temelimab, immunoadsorption compared with a sham procedure, and repurposed drugs that brought only a small benefit that did not last. ([Fluge 2019](https://doi.org/10.7326/M18-1451); [Baden 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00406-8); [Nehme 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2026.106892); [Stortz 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101744); [Wall 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00242-2))
- Exercise programmes can ease some symptoms but do not improve physical capacity, and patients rate graded exercise therapy as least helpful. In a Charité trial, better-organised care alone did not improve function. ([Zhao 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychores.2026.112677); [Eckey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2426874122); [Peters 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2026.09.013))
- Trials often miss what patients actually use. Only about 1 in 14 registered post-COVID trials tests a treatment that patients rely on, and few use PEM as the main outcome. ([Gabriel 2026](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10851936/v1))
