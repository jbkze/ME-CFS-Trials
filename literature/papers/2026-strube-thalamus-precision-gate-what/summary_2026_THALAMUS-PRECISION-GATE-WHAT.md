# ME/CFS and the thalamus as a precision gate: what reversible thalamocortical connectivity reveals about central gain in post-infectious fatigue

## Related files
- [[literature/papers/2026-strube-thalamus-precision-gate-what/citation.bib]]

## Summary

This is a commentary (an expert opinion piece, not a new experiment) that builds on a recent Charité study showing that a deep brain relay station called the thalamus—which filters and passes along sensory and body signals—works differently in ME/CFS patients, and partly shifted toward a healthy pattern after hyperbaric oxygen therapy (breathing pure oxygen in a pressurized chamber). The author proposes that many ME/CFS symptoms that seem unrelated—oversensitivity to light/sound/touch, pain, dizziness on standing, and brain fog—could all be explained by this same brain 'gatekeeper' being turned up too high, rather than being separate problems.

## Why this matters

Offers a single, unifying brain-based explanation for ME/CFS's very varied symptoms, which could help researchers design treatments that target this one control point instead of treating each symptom separately—though it's an opinion piece built on one earlier study, not new patient data, so the idea still needs direct testing.
