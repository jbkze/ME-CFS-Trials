## Summary

In a hand-grip strength test, people with ME/CFS and healthy people could squeeze just as hard at their very strongest, but the ME/CFS patients tired out much sooner. Brain and muscle activity recordings showed that as healthy people got tired, their brain and muscle signals ramped up to compensate — but in ME/CFS patients, this compensating brain signal barely increased at all.

## Why this matters

Suggests the fatigue in ME/CFS starts in how the brain drives and compensates for tired muscles, not in the muscles themselves — pointing researchers toward the brain and nervous system, not just the body, when looking for treatments for exhaustion and post-exertional crashes.
