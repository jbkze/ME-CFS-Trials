Abstract
Background  Post-COVID-19 syndrome is a major long-term sequela of severe SARS-CoV-2 infection, potentially involv­
ing persistent angiogenic and thromboinflammatory dysfunction, though long-term biomarker behavior remains unclear.
Objective  To evaluate the evolution of angiogenic and thromboinflammatory biomarkers in post-COVID-19 syndrome 
patients.
Methods  This ambispective cohort study was conducted at the National Institute of Respiratory Diseases in Mexico 
City. Thirty-two adults hospitalized for severe or critical COVID-19 in 2020 were followed for three and a half years. 
Paired plasma samples were collected during hospitalization and at long-term follow-up. Endothelial dysfunction mark­
ers included soluble P-selectin, vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2, vascular endothelial growth factor D, and 
angiopoietin-1. Hemostasis was assessed by prothrombin time. Acute-phase proteins alpha-2-macroglobulin and hapto­
globin were measured via immunoassay. Persistent symptoms were documented at follow-up.
Results  At three and a half years, persistent symptoms were common: fatigue in sixty-five point 6%, dyspnea in sixty-two 
point 5%, and concentration difficulties in sixty-five point 6%. Vascular endothelial growth factor D and angiopoietin-1 
remained elevated. Alpha-2-macroglobulin stayed high, and prothrombin time was prolonged in a subset of patients.
Conclusions  Severe COVID-19 survivors show sustained angiogenic dysregulation and thromboinflammatory imbal­
ance. Elevated vascular endothelial growth factor D and angiopoietin-1 indicate ongoing angiogenic perturbations, while 
increased alpha-2-macroglobulin and prolonged prothrombin time reflect persistent coagulation disturbances. These bio­
markers may help identify long-term vascular alterations in post-COVID-19 syndrome.

Introduction
The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic has 
resulted in a substantial global health burden, not only from 
acute infection but also from long-term sequelae affecting 
a subset of patients. Post-COVID-19 syndrome (PCS), 
also known as long COVID, is characterized by the per­
sistence of symptoms such as fatigue, dyspnea, and cogni­
tive disturbances for weeks or months after the resolution 
of acute SARS-CoV-2 infection [1]. The pathophysiology 
of PCS is complex and remains incompletely understood; 
however, accumulating evidence supports a central role 
for persistent thromboinflammatory processes, endothelial 
dysfunction, and dysregulated angiogenic signaling [2].
Endothelial dysfunction is a hallmark of acute COVID-
19, driven by direct viral injury and an exaggerated inflam­
matory response [3]. It manifests as impaired vasodilation, 
increased vascular permeability, and a prothrombotic state, 
contributing to the high incidence of thromboembolic 
complications observed in severe disease [4]. In parallel, 
SARS-CoV-2 infection has been associated with altera­
tions in angiogenic pathways, including imbalances in 
pro- and anti-angiogenic mediators, which may promote 
aberrant vascular remodeling and microvascular injury. 
While some studies suggest partial resolution of these 
abnormalities, others indicate that endothelial and angio­
genic disturbances may persist for months or years, poten­
tially contributing to the chronic manifestations of PCS 
[5].
Hemostatic abnormalities are also well documented 
in acute COVID-19, including elevated D-dimer levels, 
platelet hyperreactivity, and alterations in coagulation 
parameters such as prothrombin time (PT) and activated 
partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) [6]. These findings 
reflect a hypercoagulable and thromboinflammatory state 
closely linked to endothelial activation and immune dys­
regulation, and are associated with an increased risk of 
thrombotic events [7]. However, the long-term trajectory 
of these thromboinflammatory alterations in PCS remains 
poorly defined, with some studies reporting normalization 
and others demonstrating persistent abnormalities, partic­
ularly in patients with severe acute disease or underlying 
comorbidities [8].
Acute-phase proteins (APPs), including C-reactive pro­
tein (CRP), ferritin, haptoglobin, and alpha-2-macroglob­
ulin, are key components of the systemic inflammatory 
response and have been associated with disease severity 
and outcomes in acute COVID-19 [9]. Beyond their role 
as inflammatory markers, several APPs regulate protease 
activity and coagulation pathways, thereby linking inflam­
mation with thromboinflammation and vascular remodel­
ing. The dynamics of these proteins in the post-acute phase 
are less well characterized, but persistent alterations may 
reflect ongoing biological processes contributing to PCS 
pathogenesis [10]. Importantly, some of these mediators 
may also influence angiogenic signaling, further support­
ing their relevance in long-term vascular alterations.
The clinical manifestations of PCS are heterogeneous 
and affect multiple organ systems. Frequently reported 
symptoms include fatigue, dyspnea, chest pain, cognitive 
impairment (“brain fog”), and psychological disturbances 
such as anxiety and depression 11. These symptoms can 
significantly impair quality of life and functional capac­
ity. The underlying mechanisms are likely multifactorial, 
involving persistent immune activation, microvascular 
dysfunction, thromboinflammation, and dysregulated 
angiogenesis [12].
Despite the growing recognition of PCS as a distinct 
clinical entity, there is a paucity of data regarding the long-
term evolution of angiogenic and thromboinflammatory 
biomarkers in affected patients [13]. Most studies have 
focused on the acute or subacute phases, with limited fol­
low-up beyond six months. A better understanding of the 
persistence and interplay of these pathways is essential to 
elucidate the pathophysiology of PCS, identify patients at 
risk for chronic complications, and guide the development 
of targeted therapeutic strategies [14, 15].
The present study aims to address this knowledge gap 
by evaluating long-term changes in biomarkers reflect­
ing angiogenic activity and thromboinflammatory pro­
cesses in a well-characterized cohort of patients with PCS. 
Specifically, we compared biomarker levels during acute 
hospitalization and at long-term follow-up (3.5 years post-
infection), and assessed their association with persistent 
clinical symptoms. Our objective is to provide insight into 
the vascular and inflammatory mechanisms underlying 
PCS and to identify candidate biomarkers associated with 
long-term outcomes in this population.
Methodology
Study design and setting
This research was conducted as an ambispective cohort 
study at the Instituto Nacional de Enfermedades Respirato­
rias (INER) in Mexico City, a national reference center for 
respiratory diseases. The primary objective was to evalu­
ate the long-term evolution of endothelial dysfunction, 
hemostatic alterations, and acute-phase protein biomarkers 
in patients diagnosed with post-COVID-19 syndrome. The 
study included both retrospective and prospective compo­
nents: clinical and laboratory data from the acute phase 
of COVID-19 hospitalization (2020) were retrieved from

institutional records, and new samples and clinical assess­
ments were collected during the follow-up period between 
2023 and 2024, corresponding to approximately 3.5 years 
post-infection. The study protocol was approved by the 
INER ethics committee, and all participants provided writ­
ten informed consent prior to enrollment.
Participants and eligibility criteria
The cohort comprised adult patients (aged ≥ 18 years) who 
had been hospitalized at INER in 2020 with a confirmed 
diagnosis of severe or critical COVID-19, as defined by 
national and World Health Organization criteria. Inclu­
sion required a positive RT-PCR test for SARS-CoV-2 and 
availability for long-term follow-up. Exclusion criteria 
included pre-existing chronic inflammatory, autoimmune, 
or hematological disorders unrelated to COVID-19, as 
well as refusal or inability to provide informed consent. 
A total of 32 patients met these criteria and were included 
in the analysis. Demographic data, including age, sex, 
and relevant comorbidities, were extracted from the insti­
tutional database. The median age of participants was 50 
years (interquartile range [IQR]: 43–60 years), with a sex 
distribution of 75% male and 25% female.
Sample collection and biomarker measurement
For each participant, two peripheral blood samples were 
collected: one during acute hospitalization in 2020 (basal 
phase, BP) and another during the post-COVID follow-up 
visit in 2023–2024 (follow-up phase, FP). Blood samples 
were obtained in EDTA tubes and processed within two 
hours. Plasma was isolated by centrifugation and stored at 
− 80 °C until analysis to preserve biomarker stability.
Endothelial dysfunction and angiogenic imbalance 
were assessed through the quantification of circulating 
angiogenic and endothelial activation markers, includ­
ing soluble P-selectin (sP-selectin), vascular endothelial 
growth factor receptor 2 (VEGF-R2), vascular endothelial 
growth factor D (VEGF-D), and angiopoietin-1 (Ang-1). 
These biomarkers were selected based on their established 
role in endothelial activation, vascular permeability, and 
angiogenic signaling. Quantification was performed using 
a validated bead-based multiplex immunoassay (Invitro­
gen™ Coagulation 6-Plex Human ProcartaPlex™ Panel; 
catalog number 1553245), following the manufacturer’s 
instructions.
To complement the evaluation of endothelial and angio­
genic pathways, acute-phase proteins associated with 
systemic inflammation and vascular response, including 
alpha-2-macroglobulin and haptoglobin, were measured 
using a bead-based multiplex immunoassay (Bio-Plex 
Pro™ Human Acute Phase Assay Panel, Bio-Rad; catalog 
number 171-A4010M).
Hemostatic parameters were assessed to characterize 
the interplay between endothelial dysfunction and coag­
ulation. Prothrombin time (PT) and thrombin time (TT) 
was measured using an automated coagulation analyzer 
(ACL TOP 500 CTS™, Werfen/Instrumentation Labora­
tory), and additional parameters such as D-dimer levels 
and platelet count were recorded when available.
All assays were performed in duplicate to ensure analyt­
ical reproducibility. Internal quality controls were included 
in each run, and laboratory personnel were blinded to clin­
ical data and outcomes.
Clinical data collection and symptom assessment
Clinical data from the acute phase were obtained from the 
INER COVID-19 database, including disease severity, 
length of hospital stay, and requirement for intensive care 
unit admission or mechanical ventilation.
During the follow-up phase, participants underwent a 
structured clinical evaluation conducted by trained phy­
sicians. A standardized questionnaire was used to assess 
persistent symptoms, categorized as cardiorespiratory 
(dyspnea, cough, odynophagia), neurocognitive (head­
ache, concentration difficulties), and systemic (fatigue, 
myalgia, arthralgia). Functional status was evaluated using 
the modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea 
scale.
All data were systematically recorded in electronic case 
report forms specifically designed for the study (Fig. 1).
Statistical analysis
Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demo­
graphic and clinical characteristics. Continuous variables 
were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or 
median with interquartile range (IQR), depending on data 
distribution, while categorical variables were presented as 
frequencies and percentages.
Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. 
Paired comparisons of biomarker levels between BP and 
FP were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test 
for non-normally distributed variables or paired t-test 
when appropriate.
Correlations between angiogenic biomarkers and persis­
tent clinical symptoms were evaluated using Spearman’s 
rank correlation coefficient. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 
was considered statistically significant.
All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS soft­
ware (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results
Study population and clinical outcomes
Our longitudinal cohort included 32 patients with severe or 
critical COVID-19 (median age 50 years, IQR: 43–60; 75% 
male) followed for a median of 3.5 years (2020–2024). The 
comorbidities observed were: 93.8% obesity, 34.4% type 
2 diabetes, and 3.1% hypertension. Persistent post-acute 
symptoms were common: 65.6% of patients reported dys­
pnea, 62.5% experienced chronic fatigue, and 65.6% had 
cognitive impairment, particularly concentration difficul­
ties, reflecting the multisystemic nature of post-COVID-19 
syndrome (Table 1).
Hemostatic profile and coagulation dynamics
Non-parametric analysis showed that prothrombin time 
(PT) was significantly prolonged during the acute hospi­
talization phase (median of 15.3  s, IQR: 14.3–15.8), and 
although improved, remained elevated at follow-up with 
a median of 11.4 s (IQR: 10.6–13.8 Wilcoxon signed-rank 
test, p < 0.001). This indicates persistent coagulation abnor­
malities beyond clinical recovery. (Fig. 2)

Inflammatory and acute-phase proteins
Alpha-2-macroglobulin levels increased significantly from a 
median of 38.7 mg/dL (IQR: 0.91–69.6) during acute illness 
to 86.8 mg/dL (IQR: 50.2–138.1) at follow-up (p < 0.001), 
indicating sustained, subclinical systemic inflammation. 
Haptoglobin remained elevated above baseline with median 
values 56.7 mg/dL (IQR: 22.6–315) of follow-up compared 
to 139.6  mg/dL (IQR: 31.4–315) during the acute phase 
(p = 0.02). (Fig. 3)
Endothelial dysfunction biomarkers
Markers of endothelial activation and vascular repair exhib­
ited distinct longitudinal patterns. Soluble P-selectin (sP-
Selectin) decreased significantly from a median of 121,700 
pg/mL (IQR: 68,900–351,000) in the acute phase to 58,000 
pg/mL (IQR: 23,000–141,000) at follow-up (p = 0.010), 
indicating partial resolution of endothelial activation.
Conversely, VEGF-D showed a marked increase from a 
median of 10.4 pg/mL (IQR: 6.5–30.6) during acute illness 
to 75.2 pg/mL (IQR: 50.4–92.8) at follow-up (p < 0.001), 
exceeding normal ranges and suggesting ongoing vascular 
injury, lymphangiogenesis and inflammation. VEGF recep­
tor 2 (VEGF-R2) showed a slight decrease from 4,730 pg/
mL (IQR: 3,200-7,550) to 4,240 pg/mL (IQR: 3,420–5,200 
p = 0.045, which may indicate persistent endothelial acti­
vation, compensatory angiogenesis and residual chronic 
inflammation.
Angiopoietin-1 exhibited a non-significant upward trend, 
with median values rising from 465 pg/mL (IQR: 235–
1,160) to 440 pg/mL (IQR: 324–586; p = 0.064), possibly 
reflecting compensatory vascular remodeling. (Fig. 4)
Clinical correlations
Spearman correlation analyses showed that VEGFR-2 lev­
els were strongly and positively correlated with fibrinogen 
(r = 0.803, p < 0.001), thrombin time (r = 0.809, p < 0.001), 
and prothrombin time (r = 0.722, p < 0.001) (Fig. 5).
In a similar pattern, VEGF-D levels were also positively 
associated with fibrinogen (r = 0.815, p < 0.001), thrombin 
time (r = 0.838, p < 0.001), and prothrombin time (r = 0.759, 
p < 0.001), suggesting a consistent link between these angio­
genic markers and coagulation parameters (Fig. 6).
Statistical analyses using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests 
confirmed that these longitudinal changes in PT, acute-
phase proteins, and angiogenic markers were significant (all 
p < 0.01). Importantly, VEGF-D concentrations correlated 
strongly with persistent dyspnea (r = 0.72, p = 0.003), while 
reductions in sP-Selectin were associated with improve­
ments in fatigue scores (r = − 0.58, p = 0.018), highlighting 
meaningful clinical-biomarker relationships.
In summary, our findings reveal persistent coagulopathy 
characterized by prolonged PT, alongside chronic endo­
theliopathy evidenced by elevated VEGF-D and dynamic 
angiopoietin-1 levels.
Discussion
This study provides evidence of persistent elevated angio­
genic proteins and hemostatic alterations, in a cohort of 
patients with PCS, 3.5 years after severe or critical COVID-
19. The findings align with previous reports demonstrating 
that endothelial injury and microvascular dysfunction are 
central to PCS pathogenesis [16]. The sustained elevation 
of VEGF-D and angiopoietin-1 suggests ongoing endo­
thelial activation or impaired repair mechanisms, which

The sustained elevation of endothelial dysfunction mark­
ers such as VEGF-D and angiopoietin-1 is particularly note­
worthy. Endothelial injury is recognized as a hallmark of 
acute COVID-19, contributing to microvascular thrombo­
sis, inflammation, and multiorgan dysfunction [21]. While 
prior studies have shown that some endothelial markers nor­
malize within months, our findings indicate that in a subset 
of patients, endothelial activation and injury may persist 
even 1 year after recovery [22]. This is in line with recent 
reports of ongoing microvascular pathology and impaired 
vascular repair in long COVID cohorts [23]. The chronic 
elevation of VEGF-D and angiopoietin-1 may reflect ongo­
ing attempts at vascular remodeling or a failure to resolve 
endothelial damage, which could contribute to the persis­
tence of symptoms such as fatigue and dyspnea [24].
In line with earlier studies demonstrating the activation 
of angiogenic pathways during and after SARS-CoV-2 
infection, the current findings corroborate those reports. 
Specifically, Philippe et al. [25] demonstrated persistent 
angiogenic abnormalities in individuals with post-COVID 
conditions, supporting the concept that dysregulated vascu­
lar remodeling may contribute to long-term disease manifes­
tations. In our cohort, sustained elevations of VEGF-D and 
angiopoietin-1 were observed up to 3.5 years after severe 
or critical COVID-19, substantially extending the duration 
of previously described angiogenic alterations. Together, 
these findings support the hypothesis that chronic endothe­
lial activation and aberrant angiogenic signaling may rep­
resent long-lasting biological hallmarks of post-COVID-19 
syndrome.
Hemostatic alterations, particularly prolonged prothrom­
bin time, were also observed in our cohort at long-term 
follow-up. Coagulation abnormalities, including elevated 
D-dimer and PT, are well documented in the acute phase 
of COVID-19 and have been associated with increased risk 
of thromboembolic events [26]. Our data suggest that these 
alterations may not fully resolve in all patients, raising con­
cerns about a sustained prothrombotic state and the poten­
tial for late thrombotic complications [27]. This finding 
supports the need for ongoing risk assessment and possibly 
extended thromboprophylaxis in selected high-risk individ­
uals, as suggested by recent guidelines [28].
The persistent elevation of acute-phase proteins, particu­
larly alpha-2-macroglobulin and haptoglobin, further sup­
ports the hypothesis of chronic low-grade inflammation in 
post-COVID-19 syndrome. These proteins are well-estab­
lished markers of systemic inflammation and have been 
linked to disease severity and prognosis in acute COVID-19 
[29]. Their sustained elevation years after infection suggests 
that immune dysregulation and unresolved inflammatory 
processes may underlie the chronic symptoms observed in 
long COVID patients [30]. This is consistent with emerging 
evidence of persistent immune activation, autoimmunity, 
and dysregulated cytokine profiles in post-acute sequelae of 
COVID-19 [31].
Originality and contribution to the field
This study is among the first to provide a comprehensive 
long-term evaluation (3.5 years) of endothelial, hemo­
static, and inflammatory biomarkers in a well-characterized 
cohort of post-COVID-19 patients. While previous research 
has largely focused on the acute and subacute phases, our 
data demonstrate that biological alterations may persist far 
beyond the initial recovery period. The identification of 
VEGF-D [32], angiopoietin-1 [33], and alpha-2-macroglob­
ulin [29] as potential prognostic markers for long-term com­
plications is novel and may inform future risk stratification 
and management strategies. By linking persistent biomarker 
abnormalities with ongoing clinical symptoms, our findings 
also advance the understanding of the pathophysiological 
mechanisms underlying post-COVID-19 syndrome and 
highlight potential therapeutic targets for intervention [34].

Limitations
Despite its strengths, this study has several limitations that 
should be acknowledged. First, the sample size is relatively 
small and derived from a single center, which may limit 
the generalizability of the findings. Second, the lack of a 
matched control group (e.g., patients with mild COVID-19 
or uninfected individuals) precludes definitive conclusions 
about the specificity of the observed biomarker changes to 
post-COVID-19 syndrome. Third, the observational design 
does not allow for causal inferences regarding the relation­
ship between biomarker alterations and clinical outcomes. 
Four, the reliance on self-reported symptoms may introduce 
recall bias, and the potential impact of comorbidities or con­
current medications was not fully explored.
Furthermore, VEGF-A, a key regulator of angiogenesis 
and vascular permeability, was not included in the bio­
marker panel; therefore, a comprehensive assessment of 
VEGF-related signaling pathways was not possible. Finally, 
while we assessed a panel of well-established biomarkers, 
other relevant pathways (e.g., autoantibodies [35], specific 
cytokines [36], or markers of autonomic dysfunction [37]) 
were not analyzed and warrant further investigation.
Implications and future directions
The persistence of endothelial dysfunction, coagulation 
abnormalities, and inflammation in post-COVID-19 patients 
has important clinical and research implications. Clinically, 
our findings support the need for long-term follow-up and 
multidisciplinary management of individuals recovering 
from severe or critical COVID-19, with particular attention 
to cardiovascular, thrombotic, and neurocognitive compli­
cations [38]. Sustained elevations of VEGF-D [32], angio­
poietin-1 [33], and alpha-2-macroglobulin highlight their 
potential utility as candidate biomarkers may facilitate early 
identification of patients at risk for chronic sequelae and 
guide personalized therapeutic strategies [39, 41].
From a clinical translation viewpoint, these results high­
light the need for larger, multicenter studies with extended 
follow-up and appropriate control groups to validate and 
expand upon our findings [40]. Mechanistic studies explor­
ing the drivers of persistent endothelial and immune activa­
tion, as well as interventional trials targeting these pathways, 
are warranted [31]. For instance, therapies aimed at restoring 
endothelial function, modulating inflammation, or address­
ing coagulation abnormalities may hold promise for alleviat­
ing the burden of post-COVID-19 syndrome [2]. Building on 
these observations, the emerging interest in VEGF-targeted 
therapies, as exemplified by the ongoing BASECOVID trial 
evaluating bevacizumab in post-COVID-19 syndrome [42], 
underscores the need for a more comprehensive charac­
terization of angiogenic pathways in long COVID. Future 
studies incorporating VEGF-A, VEGF-D33, VEGF recep­
tor signaling, and other angiogenic mediators may further 
clarify the therapeutic relevance of angiogenic dysregula­
tion in PCS. Furthermore, integrating biomarker profiling 
with advanced imaging and functional assessments could 
provide a more comprehensive understanding of the multi­
systemic impact of long COVID.
In summary, our study contributes to the growing body 
of evidence that post-COVID-19 syndrome is a complex, 
chronic condition with significant clinical and biologi­
cal sequelae. The persistence of endothelial, hemostatic, 
and inflammatory abnormalities years after acute infection 
underscores the need for ongoing vigilance and innovation 
in both clinical care and research. By characterizing the 
long-term dynamics of key angiogenic and thromboinflam­
matory biomarkers, this study provides further evidence that 
persistent vascular dysfunction may contribute to the long-
term consequences of severe COVID-19. These findings 
underscore the need for larger prospective studies to eluci­
date the mechanisms driving sustained vascular, inflamma­
tory, and coagulation-related alterations following infection. 
Ultimately, a deeper understanding of these pathways may 
enhance risk assessment and support the identification of 
novel therapeutic targets for patients with PCS.
Conclusions
Our study demonstrates that a substantial proportion of 
patients with post-COVID-19 syndrome exhibit persistent 
angiogenic and thromboinflammatory alterations more than 
three years after severe or critical COVID-19. Sustained 
elevations of VEGF-D and angiopoietin-1 suggest ongoing 
dysregulation of angiogenic pathways, whereas increased 
alpha-2-macroglobulin levels and prolonged prothrombin 
time indicate persistent disturbances in inflammatory and 
coagulation-related processes. These biological abnormali­
ties correlate with a substantial burden of persistent symp­

COVID-19 syndrome. Additionally, VEGF-D, angiopoi­
etin-1, and alpha-2-macroglobulin could serve as valuable 
biomarkers for these changes. Further research is needed 
to clarify these findings and explore their potential clinical 
implications.
