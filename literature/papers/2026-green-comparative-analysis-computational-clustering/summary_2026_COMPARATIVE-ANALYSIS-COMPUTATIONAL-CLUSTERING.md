## Summary

Patient-researchers analysed survey answers from over 6,000 people with long COVID about 162 symptoms, using three different computer clustering methods to sort patients into subtypes. The three methods largely disagreed with each other about what the 'real' subtypes were, suggesting long COVID's symptom pattern is more of a continuous spectrum than a set of clear-cut categories — though people with more overall symptoms consistently also had worse post-exertional malaise (the crash after activity) and were more often women.

## Why this matters

A caution flag for the whole field of long-COVID/ME-CFS 'subtyping' research: which subtype a patient gets assigned may depend more on which statistical method a study happens to use than on a real biological grouping — which matters for how future trials pick and interpret patient subgroups.
