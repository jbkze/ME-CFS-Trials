# Registered interventional trials fail to cover patient-prioritised treatments in Post-COVID condition (PCC)

## Related files
- [[literature/papers/2026-gabriel-registered-interventional-trials-fail/citation.bib]]

## Summary

Researchers compared 714 registered post-COVID studies (from ClinicalTrials.gov, the German registry DRKS, and the EU registry) against a list of 29 treatments that patients and their doctors say they actually rely on day to day. Only about 1 in 14 of those studies tested any of those patient-favoured treatments. Post-exertional malaise (PEM, the crash after activity that is the hallmark of ME/CFS) was assessed at all in 57 trials, but was the main thing being measured in only 8 of them. Ten common treatment types — for nervous-system regulation problems, allergic-type mast-cell reactions, and certain repurposed nerve drugs — had no registered trial anywhere, and Germany's DRKS registry had zero trials matching any patient-priority treatment.

## Why this matters

Backs up, with hard numbers, a pattern this tracker keeps finding: solid drug trials aimed at the ME/CFS-like PEM symptom are rare, especially in Germany. It makes a data-driven case for steering funding — including Germany's new decade-long "Nationale Dekade" research programme — toward the treatments patients and doctors already believe help, rather than only mechanism-focused studies.

## Notes

Jena University Hospital group (Gabriel/Puta/Katzer/Reuken/Stallmach). Meta-research/landscape-analysis preprint, not new patient biology: deduplicated 854 registry records (ClinicalTrials.gov, DRKS, EU-CTR; snapshot 16 Jul 2026) into 714 unique interventional PCC trials, cross-referenced against 29 patient-guide treatments; 51/714 (7.1%) covered any guide treatment, 20 active; PEM captured in 57 trials but co-primary endpoint in only 8; 10 guide treatments (autonomic-dysfunction first-line agents, mast-cell stabilisers, several repurposed neuromodulators) had zero registered trials; DRKS contributed no guide-relevant trial.
