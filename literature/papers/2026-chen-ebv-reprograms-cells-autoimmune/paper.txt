Abstract 
17 
Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) reprograms B cells in autoimmune disease. Reprogrammed 
18 
EBV+ B cells activate nearby B and CD4+ T cells, via upregulated antigen presentation 
19 
and costimulatory machinery, to drive autoimmune pathology. EBV reactivation is a known 
20 
correlate of long COVID, which is a heterogeneous condition that can bear similarities to 
21 
autoimmune disease. However, the mechanisms underpinning this association remain 
22 
unresolved. We report on EBV metabolically reprogrammed B cells in patients with 
23 
COVID-19. We find EBV+ B cells provide stimulatory signals to bystander B and CD4+ T 
24 
cells. SARS-CoV-2 infected participants exhibiting elevated fractions of EBV+ B cells 
25 
present, at convalescence, with dysregulated lipid profiles, increased autoantibody titers, 
26 
and post-acute symptomology likely reflective of this metabolic reprogramming and cell-
27 
cell interactions. Enrichment of our EBV+ B cell signatures seen in patients with COVID-
28 
19 is similar in patients with lupus and multiple sclerosis suggesting a potentially shared 
29 
pathway of EBV-driven dysfunction across diseases.

31 
Introduction 
32 
Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) is a human gamma herpesvirus that establishes lifelong latency 
33 
with intermittent reactivation to lytic replication. EBV causes infectious mononucleosis1 in 
34 
a minority with acute infection, and is a well-known oncogenic virus, associated with 
35 
multiple cancers (e.g. Burkitt lymphoma2, gastric cancer3 and others4), and is reported to 
36 
have infected ≥90% of the global population. In the past decade, large-scale studies have 
37 
demonstrated a consistent positive association between prior EBV infection and 
38 
increased autoimmune disease risk, particularly for multiple sclerosis (MS)5–7. These 
39 
efforts have proposed molecular mimicry (similarity between human and EBV antigens) 
40 
and EBV transcription factor (e.g. EBNA2) activity as two potential mechanisms driving 
41 
EBV-associated autoimmune disease pathology8–10. Recent works have compounded on 
42 
these findings by demonstrating a fundamental reprogramming of EBV-infected B cells 
43 
towards a metabolically-distinct cell state11–14 (e.g. increased glycolysis) that markedly 
44 
upregulates antigen presentation and co-stimulation machinery15–18. Reprogrammed 
45 
EBV-infected B cells can then leverage this machinery to activate autoreactive B and

3 
Long COVID is a debilitating post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection that continues 
4 
to affect hundreds of millions of people worldwide. An estimated 7% of adults in the United 
5 
States alone have had long COVID (as reported in 2024), with global numbers estimated 
6 
as even higher 19,20. Patients with long COVID can be afflicted with a range of symptoms, 
7 
including brain fog, post-exertional malaise, chronic fatigue, respiratory, digestive, and/or 
8 
cardiovascular issues that can severely limit their quality of life19,21,22. To address this 
9 
patient hardship, many studies have sought to identify long COVID risk factors shared 
10 
across patient populations, which may thus represent potential therapeutic targets. These 
11 
studies have consistently revealed a marked positive association between long COVID 
12 
risk and EBV reactivation (or detection via viremia)23–28; these findings have been 
13 
increasingly validated in larger patient populations such as the IMPACC cohort29. While 
14 
these works establish a clear associative link between EBV and long COVID, they do not 
15 
identify underpinning molecular mechanisms. The similarity between certain 
16 
immunopathology in long COVID (e.g. dysregulated T cells and B cells and 
17 
autoantibodies30) and recently identified EBV driven mechanisms of disease in systemic 
18 
lupus erythematous (SLE) and MS raise the possibility of EBV infection as not only a 
19 
correlate of long COVID but an active effector that drives the condition’s pathology in at 
20 
least a fraction of patients. 
21

Results 
23 
Identification of EBV+ B cells in patients with COVID-19 
24 
We leveraged a reported computational framework in autoimmune disease16,17 to identify 
25 
EBV-infected B cells in a primary cohort of 209 patients with COVID-19 (INCOV) and a 
26 
validation cohort of 100 patients with COVID-19 (HAARVI)25 (Fig. 1A, Methods); the 
27 
INCOV cohort was used for the majority of analyses unless otherwise specified.  
28 
 
29 
Detected EBV reads from single-cell RNA-seq (scRNAseq) of peripheral mononuclear 
30 
blood cells (PBMCs), largely mapped to the EBNA2 gene (Figs. 1B-C). EBNA2 is a well-
31 
known transcriptional activator expressed during latency III of EBV infection31 and 
32 
reported to bind at transcription-start-sites of genes involved in B cell survival, activation, 
33 
and antigen presentation16,32,33. EBV+ B cells were initially identified using a strict cutoff 
34 
for reads that uniquely mapped to the EBV genome (Fig. 1D); this revealed an average 
35 
of 0.17±0.06% EBV+ B cells. As viruses bear highly repetitive genomes34, viral reads often 
36 
map multiple times to their genome and are thus lost when using strict cutoffs that require 
37 
unique mapping. To overcome this, we also assigned B cells with viral reads that mapped 
38 
multiple times to the EBV genome as EBV+. We confirmed the validity of this approach 
39 
by finding all EBV+ B cells to enrich for an EBV-specific gene signature regardless of the 
40 
number of times their EBV reads mapped to the EBV genome (Fig. S1). This resulted in 
41 
an average of 2.76±0.27% EBV+ B cells across patients and healthy donors (Table S1). 
42 
These EBV+ assignments were subsequently utilized for downstream analyses. Notably, 
43 
few EBV+ B cells could be detected in healthy donors in comparison with patients with 
44 
COVID-19. This finding is consistent with prior reports of EBV reactivation after SARS-
45 
.

3 
EBV transcriptionally reprograms B cells in patients with COVID-19 
4 
Inspired by recent reports of marked B cell reprogramming by EBV in autoimmune 
5 
disease16–18, we hypothesized that EBV+ B cells may undergo a similar reprogramming in 
6 
patients with COVID-19. To investigate this, we first classified single B cells into canonical 
7 
B cell states by their marker gene expression (e.g. FCER2 in naïve B cells35) (Figs. 2A 
8 
and S2A). We then interrogated for differences in the distribution of B cell states in EBV+ 
9 
versus EBV- B cell compartments. This revealed EBV+ B cells to be significantly biased 
10 
towards memory B cell and plasmablast states (Fig. 2B) and is reminiscent of previous 
11 
findings of EBV-transformed B cells as highly proliferative and plasmablast-like36. EBV+ 
12 
naïve B cells suggests recent infection from viral particles released by EBV-infected B 
13 
cells that have undergone completion of a reactivated lytic cycle37. 
14 
 
15 
To identify the exact transcriptional modules reprogrammed by EBV, we performed 
16 
differential gene expression analysis between EBV+ and EBV- B cells (Fig. 2C, Table S2). 
17 
Interestingly, we observed EBV+ B cells to present with more significantly upregulated 
18 
(N=30, FDR<0.05) rather than downregulated genes (N=3, FDR<0.05). This is consistent 
19 
with the expression of the EBV-derived transcriptional activator EBNA2 in EBV+ B cells. 
20 
Transcription factor (TF) program enrichment analysis revealed EBV+ B cells to activate 
21 
well-known EBV-controlled TFs (Figs. 2D and S2B, Table S3), such as MYC and SPI1 
22 
which drive B cell proliferation and interact with EBV proteins (e.g. EBNA2)38–40. Pathway 
23 
enrichment analysis revealed a distinct reprogramming of EBV+ B cells towards increased 
24 
antigen presentation, energy production via glycolysis, and B cell activation and survival 
25 
signaling (Fig. 2E, Table S4). Consistently, EBV has been reported to upregulate 
26 
glycolytic11,41 and anti-apoptotic functions42,43 and drives increased antigen presentation 
27 
in autoimmune disease16,18. Increased B cell activation in EBV+ B cells was confirmed by 
28 
kinase enrichment analysis revealing increased BTK, SYK, and ZAP70 activity44–46 (Table 
29 
S5). In conjunction with the enrichment of EBV infection associated genes in EBV+ B cells, 
30 
these data suggest that patients with COVID-19 experience bona fide EBV 
31 
reprogramming of their B cells towards a distinct transcriptional cell state. We confirmed 
32 
these findings of EBV reprogrammed B cells in our independent validation cohort of 100 
33 
patients with COVID-19 (HAARVI) (Figs. S2C-D). 
34 
 
35 
EBV reprogrammed B cells in COVID-19 resemble those in autoimmune disease 
36 
The enrichment of antigen presentation and B cell activation processes in EBV 
37 
reprogrammed B cells in the context of COVID-19 is reminiscent of the EBV 
38 
reprogramming observed in certain autoimmune diseases16,18. This is supported by the 
39 
significant enrichment of autoimmune disease related genes (e.g. SLE) in EBV+ B cells 
40 
(Table S10). To formally investigate this similarity, we queried for the expression of genes 
41 
(e.g. CD27, CD70, IL2RG, IGHA1, PTPN22) and functions (e.g. proteasome, antigen 
42 
presentation, interferon and B cell activation signaling) reported to be specifically 
43 
upregulated in EBV reprogrammed B cells in autoimmune disease11,16–18. These markers 
44 
were, by and large, all significantly upregulated in EBV+ B cells in patients with COVID-
45 
19 (Figs. 3A and S3A). We confirmed these observations by finding EBV+ B cells in 
46

13

14

EBV metabolically reprograms memory B cells in COVID-19 
19 
The enrichment of public EBV+ memory B cell gene signatures in the EBV+ B cells 
20 
identified in this study (Fig. 3B) aligns with the known preference of EBV to remain latent 
21 
in memory B cells. Consistently, in this study we found EBV+ memory B cells to present 
22 
with the greatest upregulation of defining EBV reprogramming genes (e.g. HLA-DRA, 
23 
HLA-DRB1 encoding for antigen presentation machinery, Fig. 3C). Notably, we also 
24 
observed significant overlap (p<0.001, by permutation) in the genes upregulated in EBV+ 
25 
B cells across B cell states (Table S11). These genes were enriched for glycolytic and 
26 
immunomodulatory functions (e.g. ENO149 and OAS150) and may suggest the existence 
27 
of a core EBV reprogramming signature. 
28 
 
29 
We then performed a targeted analysis of memory B cells, given their importance in EBV 
30 
pathology. EBV+ memory B cells presented with a marked shift in phenotype upregulating 
31 
genes related to B cell activation (e.g. BTK44, NFKB151) and costimulation (e.g. CD80, 
32 
CD8652) (Figs. 3D and S4A-B). The increased metabolic demands of cell activation, and 
33 
subsequent proliferation, aligns with the upregulation of energy production pathways we 
34 
observed in Figs. 2B and S2D. Together, this led us to hypothesize that EBV+ B cells may 
35 
also undergo metabolic reprogramming, in the context of COVID-19. To address this, we 
36 
performed metabolic flux analysis and found EBV+ memory B cells to significantly 
37 
upregulate flux through the glycolysis pathway and decrease fatty acid breakdown (i.e. 
38 
beta-oxidation) (Fig. 3E, Table S12). 
39 
 
40

41 
plasma metabolite levels. We assessed for such dysregulation in patients with increased 
42 
proportions of EBV+ B cells and were indeed able to find sets of metabolites whose levels 
43 
during convalescence (2-3 months after infection) significantly correlated with patient 
44 
EBV+ B cell percentages (Fig. S4C, Table S13). The enrichment of these metabolites for 
45 
dicarboxylic acids and carnitines, especially those of longer lengths, is consistent with our

EBV reprogrammed B cells provide stimulatory input to B and CD4+ T cells 
13 
The upregulation of costimulatory molecules in EBV+ B cells is reminiscent of reported 
14 
mechanisms in autoimmune disease whereby EBV+ B cells activate autoreactive B and 
15 
CD4+ T cells. This activation can lead to autoimmune pathology through, for example, the 
16 
production of autoantibodies16,55,56. In conjunction with the shared clinical presentations 
17 
of long COVID and autoimmune disease57–60, these data raise the possibility of shared 
18 
molecular mechanisms between these conditions. To investigate this possibility, we 
19 
queried for cell-cell interactions between EBV+ B cells and bystander EBV- B and CD4+ T 
20 
cells in patients with COVID-19 (Table S14). Strikingly, we observed EBV+ B cells to be 
21 
significantly enriched for ligand-receptor interactions that facilitate B cell survival and 
22 
activation (e.g. BAFF:BAFFR, BAFF:TACI)61–65 and CD4+ T cell co-stimulation (e.g. 
23 
CD80:CD28, CD86:CD2866,67, CD70:CD2768–70) (Figs. 4A-B and S5). In line with the role 
24 
of this mechanism in autoimmune disease, we found patients with increased EBV+ B cell 
25 
percentages to present with increased serum levels of autoantibodies (Fig. 4C) and 
26 
increased symptomology 2-3 months after acute disease resolution (Fig. S6). Thus, in 
27 
the context of COVID-19, EBV+ B cells appear to facilitate B and CD4+ T cell expansion 
28 
through the expression of pro-survival and stimulatory ligands; this activation may 
29 
facilitate the production of autoantibodies that can contribute to, certain, long COVID 
30 
pathology (Fig. 4D)71. 
31 
 
32 
COVID-19 EBV+ B cell signature is enriched in patients with autoimmune disease 
33 
The molecular similarities between the EBV+ B cells in this work and EBV+ B cells in 
34 
autoimmune disease6,16,17,72 suggest the existence of a core EBV reprogramming module 
35 
across conditions (Fig. S7A). To investigate this, we interrogated for similarities between 
36 
EBV+ B cells in COVID and in SLE, an autoimmune condition that shares some clinical 
37 
features with long COVID57–60. We found the EBV+ B cell signature in COVID to 
38 
significantly positively correlate with the expression of genes implicated in SLE (RNA, 
39 
transcriptomic) and for patient EBV+ B cell percentages to significantly positively correlate 
40 
with their polygenic SLE risk (DNA, genomic) (Fig. 5A). We confirmed these findings by 
41 
showing our EBV+ B cell signatures in the COVID cohort to be significantly enriched in a 
42 
public cohort of patients with SLE73 (Figs. 5B-C and S7B-C, Table S15). As EBV 
43 
reprogramming is also implicated in MS, we repeated this analysis in a public cohort of 
44 
patients with MS74 and similarly found enrichment of our EBV+ B cell signatures from the 
45 
COVID cohort (Fig. 5D).

Discussion 
2 
In this work, we have identified EBV+ B cells in patients with COVID-19, even 2-3 months 
3 
after acute disease resolution, and revealed how they are transcriptionally and 
4 
metabolically reprogrammed to facilitate not only their own activation but also the 
5 
activation of nearby EBV- B and CD4+ T cells through potent stimulatory signals (Fig. 6). 
6 
To this end, we have four fundamental observations. First, the identification of a non-zero 
7 
(non-amplified) transcript signal within a B cell that is assignable to the EBV EBNA2 gene 
8 
is sufficient to identify that cell as EBV-reprogrammed. Second, that reprogramming, 
9 
investigated here within the context of COVID-19, strongly resembles EBV reprogrammed 
10 
B cells in autoimmune disease and is indeed associated with increased autoantibody 
11 
titers and long COVID symptomology (e.g. difficulty concentrating). Third, the scRNAseq-
12 
assessed level of B cell reprogramming is at least semiquantitative; the impact in patients 
13 
with a higher % of EBV transformed B cells is measurably greater. Finally, reprogrammed 
14 
B cells can exert significant influence over the global plasma environment of an individual. 
15 
 
16 
Our finding that EBV+ B cells signatures in COVID-19 are similar to those observed in 
17 
autoimmune disease, at the transcriptomic and epigenomic level (Figs. 2C-E, S2B-D, 
18 
3A-B, and S3), suggests the existence of a core EBV reprogramming gene module 
19 
shared across diseases. This hypothesis is supported by the enrichment of certain 
20 
transcriptional programs observed in this work and autoimmune disease in EBV-driven 
21 
malignancies and lymphoproliferative disorders (e.g. MYC and NFkB signaling, apoptosis 
22 
inhibition)11,75. This core module may be refined and leveraged by future works to 1) 
23 
identify additional conditions where EBV reprogramming plays a role and 2) identify 
24 
potential therapeutic targets expressed across EBV-associated pathologies. Further, the 
25 
interrogation of gene expression changes in EBV+ B cells, by this work and others, will 
26 
better our understanding of genes recently implicated as increasing risk EBV infection, 
27 
and subsequent elevated risk of autoimmune disease and malignancies76,77. For example, 
28 
decreased expression of FOXO1 in EBV+ B cells after COVID-19 may align with the 
29 
known repression of that gene by EBV LMP1/2 proteins (Fig. S7D)78. EBV+ B cell 
30 
upregulation of NFKB1 and TNFRSF13B proposes these genes as positive regulators of 
31 
EBV persistence, potentially through their facilitation of B cell survival signaling62,63. As 
32 
EBV+ B cells are more broadly identified and characterized in future studies, the cross-
33 
disease sharing and disease-specific roles of these genes will become ever more clear. 
34 
 
35 
We reveal EBV+ B cell signatures in COVID-19 as similar to those in autoimmune disease 
36 
at the transcriptomic level and in terms of their cell-cell communication profiles (Figs. 3A-
37 
B and S5). Recent works on SLE and MS demonstrated EBV+ B cells as drivers of 
38 
autoimmune pathology by facilitating the expansion of both themselves and autoreactive 
39 
double negative 2 (DN2, IgD-CD27-) and memory B cells into autoantibody secreting 
40 
plasmablasts, secondary to stimulatory ligand-receptor interactions16,36,79,80. Here, we find 
41 
these same interactions to be present between EBV+ B cells and EBV- B and CD4+ T cells 
42 
in patients with COVID-19. These interactions are an attractive explanation for the 
43 
increased autoantibody levels observed in patients with COVID-19 with increased EBV+ 
44 
B cell percentages (Fig. 3C). This explanation is supported by evidence of COVID-19 
45 
DN2 cells as potent secretors of autoantibodies30 and the observed persistence of EBV+ 
46

2

7

8

9 
 
10 
We also find elevated fractions of EBV+ B cells to be associated with increased levels of 
11 
both long-chain carnitine and dicarboxylic acid levels (Figs. 3F and S4C-F). These 
12 
metabolite levels may be associated with the biology of EBV-driven B cell reprogramming, 
13 
that includes increased glycolysis and reduced beta-oxidation. Impaired beta-oxidation, 
14 
often secondary to mitochondrial stress, can lead to elevated levels of carnitines and 
15 
dicarboxylic acids53,54,82,83. This accumulation can occur due to a build-up of reactants 
16 
(e.g. fatty acid associated carnitines) and shunting towards omega-oxidation, which 
17 
produces dicarboxylic acids as a product. Future metabolically-focused studies may 
18 
explore the exact processes that underpin this metabolic stress and provide deeper 
19 
characterizations of the alternate pathways (e.g. omega-oxidation) that are activated as 
20 
a result. The observation of serum metabolite changes as a reflection of cellular 
21 
dysfunction is not without precedent. Certain inborn errors of metabolism (e.g. medium-
22 
chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency) that lead to cellular dysfunction can be 
23 
diagnosed through plasma metabolite profiles, such as the distribution of carnitines84,85. 
24 
It will be important for future studies to validate these findings in larger cohorts with more 
25 
comprehensive metabolic panels. Positive reports from these validation studies would 
26 
support the use of serum metabolite levels as a biomarker for certain types of long COVID 
27 
and EBV-associated disease and propose the treatments used for similar congenital 
28 
metabolic disorders as potential therapeutics for these conditions. 
29 
 
30 
Together this work synergizes with existing literature to propose a potentially addressable 
31 
mechanism of long COVID: EBV+ B cell driven production of autoantibodies, recently 
32 
reported to be causative agents of long COVID symptoms71,86, and concomitant immune 
33 
cell and metabolic dysregulation, potentially secondary to interactions with EBV+ B cells. 
34 
Together, these data suggest the clearance of EBV+ B cells, or disruption of their signaling, 
35 
as a potential therapy for long COVID and, given potentially shared molecular 
36 
mechanisms and clinical features, autoimmune disease. 
37 
 
38 
Limitations of the Study 
39 
This study is limited by the number of identified EBV+ B cells. Our approach leveraged 
40 
scRNAseq data that did not undergo amplification for EBV reads (via EBV-specific 
41 
primers). This has advantages in terms of extracting quantitative relationships, but only 
42 
cells with markedly high expression of EBV transcripts could be assigned as EBV+. 
43 
Further, due to the limited number of EBV+ B cells in healthy donors, we were restrained 
44 
in our ability to comment on the EBV reprogramming differences between healthy donors 
45 
and patients with COVID-19. This may be elaborated in future works with larger cohorts 
46

Methods 
1 
Identification of EBV+ cells from scRNAseq data 
2 
EBV read detection was performed on two public cohorts of patients with COVID-19 and 
3 
healthy controls (INCOV, HAARVI)25. EBV reads were identified using a previously 
4 
published computational framework16. In brief, binary alignment map (BAM) files were 
5 
retrieved from standard 10x Genomics cellranger output (“possorted_genome_bam.bam”) 
6 
and filtered for unmapped reads (i.e. those not mapped to the human genome) via 
7 
SAMtools (v1.23.1)87 with “samtools view -b -f 4 INPUT_FILE > OUTPUT_FILE”. Non-
8 
human reads were then mapped against the EBV genome (NCBI reference sequence: 
9 
NC_007605.1). EBV gene annotations, in gene transfer format (GTF), were refined as 
10 
per Younis et al.16 and Grande and Gerhard et al.88. Read mapping was performed using 
11 
Spliced Transcripts Alignment to a Reference (STAR, v2.7.10a)89 with parameter values 
12 
set to those defined by Younis et al.16; cell barcode whitelists were downloaded from 10x 
13 
Genomics (“737K-august-2016.txt” was utilized for the 10x chemistry utilized in this work). 
14 
Cell barcodes were gathered from reads that aligned to the EBV genome and visualized 
15 
via the Integrative Genomics Viewer (IGV, v2.11.9)90 after deepTools (v3.5.6)91 
16 
processing. 
17 
 
18 
Assignment of B cell states 
19 
Single B cell transcriptome UMAP positions and unbiased clustering assignments 
20 
(calculated via the leiden92 algorithm) were previously reported25. B cell marker genes 
21 
were utilized to annotate unbiased clusters into B cell states: FCER2 (encoding CD23) 
22 
and IGHD (encoding IgD) for naïve B cells, IGHM (encoding IgM) for unswitched memory 
23 
B cells, MS4A1 (encoding CD20) for non-plasmablast B cells, ZEB2 and FCRL5 for 
24 
atypical memory (double negative 2) B cells, and XBP1 and CD38 for plasmablasts. B 
25 
cell states that were heterogenous (composed of multiple unbiased clusters) were 
26 
separated with a numerical suffix (e.g. Naïve I, Naïve II, Naïve III). 
27 
 
28 
Differential gene expression analysis 
29 
Differentially expressed genes were identified through pairwise comparisons between the 
30 
expression of each gene in group A (e.g. EBV+ B cells) and group B (e.g. EBV- B cells). 
31 
Expression values were normalized by library size (counts per million plus a pseudocount 
32 
for one, as previously reported25) and a log-transformation (natural base). Normalized 
33 
values are utilized for all statistical analyses, imputed (by MAGIC93) expression was only 
34 
utilized for visualization purposes on single cell UMAPs. P-values were calculated using 
35 
the Mann-Whitney U test and corrected for multiple hypothesis testing using the 
36 
Benjamin-Hochberg method (also called False Discovery Rate or FDR). 
37

Process98. Kinase enrichment analysis was performed using ARCHS499. Disease

Metabolic flux and metabolomics analysis 
28 
Single cell metabolic flux analysis was performed using scFEA105. scFEA provides 
29

30

31

32 
performed on previously reported Metabolon data25. We identified metabolites associated 
33 
with increased proportions of EBV+ B cells in patient B cell compartments via correlation 
34 
analysis (Pearson’s method). This was performed between a patient’s maximum EBV+ B 
35 
cell percentages cross timepoints and timepoint specific metabolite levels in plasma. The 
36 
EBV+ B cell percentages described here were utilized for all patient level comparisons 
37 
(e.g. with maximum autoantibody levels in patient serum).

12 
Comparisons to autoimmune disease were performed by quantifying the expression of 
13

19

20

22

25

26

27

28

29

30

32

33 
typical scRNAseq processing: filtering for high-quality cells (≥1000 unique molecular 
34 
identifiers per cell, ≥500 unique genes per cell, <10% mitochondrial reads per cell) and 
35 
removal of doublets (via Scrublet114) with a maximum doublet score set to 0.25. B cells 
36 
were identified as unbiased clusters (calculated via the leiden112 algorithm) with 
37 
upregulation of B cell marker genes CD79A and CD79B. Unbiased clustering was 
38 
performed on a k-nearest-neighbors graph calculated from principal component analysis 
39 
(PCA) dimensions; PCA was performed on highly variable genes as defined by 
40

41
