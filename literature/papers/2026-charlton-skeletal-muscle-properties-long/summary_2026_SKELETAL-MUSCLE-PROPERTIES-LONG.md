# Skeletal muscle properties in long COVID and ME/CFS differ from those induced by bed rest

## Summary

Researchers compared muscle biopsies and exercise tests from people with long COVID and ME/CFS against healthy volunteers who had done 60 days of strict bed rest (used to mimic pure physical deconditioning). The long-COVID and ME/CFS patients had fewer small blood vessels in their muscles and more 'fast' muscle fibres that tire quickly — a pattern that did not match what bed rest alone produces — and the ME/CFS patients also showed extra loss of their fatigue-resistant ('slow') muscle fibres.

## Why this matters

Pushes back against the idea that ME/CFS and long-COVID muscle problems are just from being sedentary, pointing instead to a real, disease-specific process in the muscle — directly relevant to Klaus Wirth's skeletal-muscle theory of post-exertional malaise (PEM, a crash after activity), even though he wasn't part of this study.
