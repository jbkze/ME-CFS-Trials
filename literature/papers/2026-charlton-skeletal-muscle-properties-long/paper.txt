Abstract 
28 
Patients with long COVID and myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) suffer 
29 
from a reduced exercise capacity, skeletal muscle abnormalities and post-exertional malaise (PEM), 
30 
where symptoms worsen with cognitive or physical exertion. PEM often results in avoidance of 
31 
physical activity, resulting in a lower aerobic fitness, which may contribute to skeletal muscle 
32 
abnormalities. Here, we compared whole-body exercise responses and skeletal muscle adaptations 
33 
after strict 60-day bed rest in healthy people with those in patients with long COVID and ME/CFS, 
34 
and healthy age- and sex-matched controls. Bed rest altered the respiratory and cardiovascular 
35 
responses to (sub)maximal exercise, while patients exhibited respiratory alterations only at 
36 
submaximal exercise. Bed rest caused muscle atrophy, and the reduced oxidative phosphorylation 
37 
related to reductions in maximal oxygen uptake. Patients with long COVID and ME/CFS did not have 
38 
muscle atrophy, but had less capillaries and a more glycolytic fibers, none of which were associated 
39 
with maximal oxygen uptake. While the whole-body aerobic capacity is similar following bed rest 
40 
compared to patients, the skeletal muscle characteristics differed, suggesting that physical inactivity 
41 
alone does not explain the lower exercise capacity in long COVID and ME/CFS.

Introduction 
44 
Although most acute SARS CoV2 infections resolve within days to weeks, some symptoms persist or 
45 
worsen in a subset of patients1–3. The continuation or development of symptoms after 12 weeks is 
46 
termed long COVID, and patients typically experience debilitating fatigue, brain fog, postural 
47 
orthostatic tachycardia, myalgia, and post-exertional malaise (PEM)3. PEM, experienced by ~90% of 
48 
patients with long COVID4, is the worsening of symptoms following physical or psychological 
49 
exertion. While the pathophysiology underlying long COVID remains unclear, recent evidence 
50 
suggests a close resemblance to myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS)5, 
51 
another disease characterized by reduced exercise capacity, brain fog and PEM. Importantly, PEM 
52 
often results in avoidance of physical activity, which may contribute to the skeletal muscle 
53 
abnormalities often observed in both conditions6–12. Exercise intensity and duration largely dictates 
54 
the magnitude of whole-body exercise13 and skeletal muscle adaptations14 ; however due to PEM, long 
55 
COVID and ME/CFS patients may not achieve such intensities or durations frequently enough to 
56 
induce such adaptations, resulting in deconditioning15–17. 
57 
Deconditioning can range from mild step reductions to strict bed rest, with more severe models 
58 
leading to more rapid reductions in aerobic capacity and skeletal muscle function18–21. These declines 
59 
are often associated with muscle atrophy, capillary rarefaction, as well as reduced mitochondrial 
60 
content and mitochondrial function22–25 , but also reduced cardiac output23,26 and maximal ventilatory 
61 
capacity27,28. However, whether these alterations cohere with those in patients with long COVID and 
62 
ME/CFS has not been directly investigated. Patients with long COVID and ME/CFS exhibit lower 
63 
aerobic exercise capacity and mitochondrial respiration than healthy individuals6,7,9,10, reduced 
64 
capillary densities9 , and some indications of muscle atrophy7, which has often been attributed to 
65 
deconditioning15,16 However, whether deconditioning alone accounts for these changes remains 
66 
unclear. 
67 
The current study aimed to compare whole body exercise responses and skeletal muscle adaptations in 
68 
patients with long COVID and ME/CFS to those observed following strict 60-day bed rest. We 
69 
hypothesized that while whole body aerobic capacity would be similarly lower between patients and 
70 
following bed rest, adaptations in skeletal muscle markers for oxygen supply and utilization would 
71 
diverge. Specifically, we expected patients to display no muscle atrophy, and reduced capillary-to-
72 
fiber ratios and capillary densities. In contrast, we expected bed rest to result in severe muscle 
73 
atrophy, reduced capillary-to-fiber ratios, and increased capillary density. Further, we expected that 
74 
muscle size and tissue markers for oxygen supply and utilization rates are associated to whole-body 
75 
exercise capacity in healthy participants undergoing bed rest, but that such association is lost in 
76 
patients. To test these hypotheses, we analysed aerobic exercise responses and vastus lateralis skeletal 
77 
muscle biopsies of patients with ME/CFS and long COVID, and compared these to age- and sex-

Discussion 
212 
The major finding of the present study is that acute exercise responses and skeletal muscle alterations 
213 
in long COVID and ME/CFS differ from those caused by bed rest-induced deconditioning per se. 
214 
Despite similar whole body exercise capacity in both patient groups and participants after bed rest, the 
215 
pulmonary and cardiovascular responses to acute exercise were distinct. Bed rest induced severe 
216 
muscle atrophy, which was not observed in patients with long COVID or ME/CFS. Instead, both 
217 
patient groups displayed a lower proportion of type I fibers, whereas only patients with ME/CFS 
218 
exhibited lower capillary-to-fiber ratios and type I specific atrophy. Further, the lower oxidative 
219 
phosphorylation capacity in both patient groups did not correlate with   , in contrast to bed rest 
220 
participants, suggesting that physiological impairments distinct from inactivity drive reduced exercise 
221 
capacity in patients. These findings challenge the notion that deconditioning is the primary driver of 
222 
reduced exercise capacity in long COVID and ME/CFS. Rather, our observations suggest intrinsic 
223 
skeletal muscle abnormalities contribute to limited exercise capacity, including reduced capillary 
224 
supply relative to muscle fiber size and mitochondrial impairments.  
225

226

227 
undergoing a strict 60-day bed rest. However, the physiological determinants underpinning this 
228 
reduction differed. Ventilatory responses to acute exercise differed following bed rest compared to the 
229 
patient groups. Bed rest resulted in reduced  , consistent with the decrease in   , whereas 
230

235

236

Skeletal Muscle Oxidative Changes 
251 
The reduction in oxidative phosphorylation capacity following bed rest was shown to be primarily 
252 
attributed to a reduction in SDH activity, often used as a proxy for mitochondrial density18,19,40–42. In 
253 
contrast, the impaired oxidative phosphorylation capacity in both patient groups was still present 
254 
following normalization to SDH activity, suggesting that intrinsic mitochondrial respiration may be 
255 
primarily affected. Furthermore, while E/L coupling efficiency tended to be reduced in both patient 
256 
groups, this was not observed following bed rest. These results suggest that skeletal muscle oxidative 
257 
impairments in long COVID and ME/CFS cannot solely be attributed to deconditioning. Indeed, 
258 
previous work suggests abnormal ultrastructure, particularly reduced cristae density, in patients with 
259 
long COVID and ME/CFS11, while bed rest is typically associated with more fragmented 
260 
mitochondria18. What the cause of the intrinsic mitochondrial alterations in long COVID and ME/CFS 
261 
is, is currently unclear. Future work is required to quantify skeletal muscle mitochondrial 
262 
ultrastructure in long COVID and ME/CFS. 
263 
Notably, oxidative phosphorylation capacity and SDH activity correlated with whole-body maximal 
264 
oxygen uptake in healthy individuals (before and after bed rest), whereas these were absent in long 
265 
COVID and ME/CFS. This suggests that factors beyond skeletal muscle mitochondrial respiration 
266 
constrain exercise capacity in long COVID and ME/CFS, including impairments in endothelial 
267 
function43–47, a reduced venous return and cardiac preload38, or a reduction in muscle diffusive 
268 
capacity48 contributing to impaired peripheral oxygen extraction39. Conversely, mitochondrial 
269 
respiration has been shown to constrain     in healthy sedentary, untrained individuals49,50, which 
270 
is consistent with the strong correlations observed between mitochondrial markers and     across 
271 
all healthy cohorts. Thus, it is likely the constraints on    imposed by bed rest, long COVID and 
272 
ME/CFS differ, implying that the reduced aerobic exercise capacity in long COVID and ME/CFS 
273 
cannot solely be ascribed to deconditioning.

290 
Capillary-to-fiber ratio is highly related to muscle FCSA, wherein larger fibers typically have more 
291 
capillary contacts to accommodate oxygen and nutrient supply, and metabolite removal52,53,58. While 
292 
these relations existed in all groups, except following bed rest, this relationship was downshifted in 
293

294 
capillaries are available to supply oxygen and nutrients for a given FCSA in patients, which is distinct 
295 
from the capillary alterations following strict bed rest22. The reduced capillary-to-fiber for a given 
296

297

298

299

301

302 
muscle cross sections (Supplemental Figure 5B). We suspected that if local oxygen supply was 
303 
reduced, there would be a compensatory increase in myoglobin content, and likewise, oxygen 
304 
extraction was impaired, myoglobin content would be reduced. However, myoglobin content in 
305 
neither patients with long COVID nor ME/CFS differed from healthy individuals. In contrast, bed rest 
306

307

309

Limitations 
332 
The current study indicates that skeletal muscle adaptations and lower aerobic exercise capacities 
333 
observed in patients with long COVID and ME/CFS are different from those occurring after bed rest, 
334 
however there are several limitations. We did not induce physical inactivity in patients with ME/CFS 
335 
or long COVID. Patients are typically more sedentary because PEM worsens both their symptoms and 
336 
skeletal muscle abnormalities6. We also cannot completely exclude the contribution of physical 
337 
inactivity to the skeletal muscle alterations observed in patients with long COVID and ME/CFS, 
338 
however our results do indicate that physical inactivity alone is insufficient to explain the skeletal 
339 
muscle changes in patients. There was likely self-selection bias towards patients with milder 
340 
symptoms, as they volunteered to travel to the laboratory for multiple occasions and undergo a 
341 
maximal exercise test. Our results are therefore not directly applicable to home-bound patients 67. 
342 
Lastly, the current research design precludes us to draw conclusions about the differences in 
343 
pathophysiology of long COVID and ME/CFS. ME/CFS patients were ill for a significantly longer 
344 
period, and therefore we cannot distinguish between disease duration and disease pathophysiology.

Biopsy Procedure 
448 
Muscle biopsies were obtained from the vastus lateralis at one-third of the distal length 
449 
(approximately 150mg weight wet) using a rongeur (4mm diameter for the bed rest cohort) or a 
450

451

452

453

454

455

456

457

461

462
