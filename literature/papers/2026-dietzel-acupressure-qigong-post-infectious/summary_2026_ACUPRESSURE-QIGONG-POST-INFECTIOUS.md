# Acupressure and Qigong in post-infectious fatigue with impaired physical function: a randomized clinical trial

## Summary

In this Charité-led trial ('ACUQiG'), 235 people with lasting fatigue and reduced physical functioning after an infection (mostly post-COVID) were split into two groups: one did 8 weeks of daily self-applied acupressure (pressing on specific body points, about 18 minutes a day) plus twice-weekly online Qigong (a gentle mind-body exercise), while the other just got a self-care information leaflet. On the main measure — physical functioning — the acupressure/Qigong group scored a bit higher after 8 weeks, but the difference was too small to be statistically certain it wasn't due to chance. A separate fatigue scale did show a small, statistically real improvement in the treatment group.

## Why this matters

A rigorously tested complementary-medicine approach, run by Carmen Scheibenbogen's own Charité group, came back essentially negative on its main goal of restoring physical function — useful, honest evidence for tempering expectations about this kind of non-drug approach, even though it showed a small effect on fatigue specifically.

## Notes

Frontiers in Medicine, vol. 13, published online Aug 10, 2026. Charité (Institute for Social Medicine, Epidemiology and Health Economics — Brinkhaus/Roll group — with Scheibenbogen and Bellmann-Strobl from the Fatigue Centrum as co-authors). RCT 'ACUQiG', n=235 (118 intervention / 117 control) with post-infectious/post-COVID fatigue and impaired physical function, 85% female, mean age 42; 8 weeks of self-applied acupressure (9 mandatory + up to 2 symptom-specific points) plus twice-weekly online Qigong vs. self-care leaflet only (both arms received written complementary-medicine guidance). Primary outcome (SF-36 Physical Functioning Subscale at 8 weeks): intervention 55.4 vs control 51.2, adjusted mean difference 4.2 (95% CI -0.9 to 9.4, p=0.109) — not statistically significant. Responder analysis (>=10-point improvement) favored intervention (58.9% vs 46.3%, OR 1.69, p=0.058) but also not significant. Chalder Fatigue Scale showed a small but statistically significant improvement (21.4 vs 23.2, p=0.040). Adherence 76.8% (acupressure) / 62.5% (Qigong). HIGH PRIORITY (Scheibenbogen-affiliated).
