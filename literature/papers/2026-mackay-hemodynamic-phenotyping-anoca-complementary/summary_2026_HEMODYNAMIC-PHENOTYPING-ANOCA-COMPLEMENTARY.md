# Hemodynamic Phenotyping in ME/CFS and ANOCA: Complementary Insights from Coronary Function Testing and Invasive Cardiopulmonary Exercise Testing

## Related files
- [[literature/papers/2026-mackay-hemodynamic-phenotyping-anoca-complementary/citation.bib]]

## Summary

Researchers tested 261 people with unexplained chest pain using detailed heart and blood-vessel tests. 18% of them also had ME/CFS, and 89% of those had "ANOCA" — a condition where the heart's tiniest blood vessels don't work properly even though the main arteries look normal. During exercise, their hearts delivered oxygen normally, but their muscles struggled to use it, pointing to a problem in the muscle rather than the heart.

## Why this matters

Diagnosing this small-vessel problem changed how doctors treated 81% of affected patients, suggesting some ME/CFS chest-pain and exercise-intolerance symptoms map onto a specific, already-recognized and testable heart condition — a concrete lead for better diagnosis and management.
