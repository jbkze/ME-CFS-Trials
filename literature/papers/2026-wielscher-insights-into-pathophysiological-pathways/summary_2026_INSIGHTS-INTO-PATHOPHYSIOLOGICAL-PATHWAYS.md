# Insights into Pathophysiological Pathways in ME/CFS Through Genetic Correlation and Mendelian Randomization

## Related files

## Summary

Using genetic data from the large DecodeME study (over 15,000 people with ME/CFS), researchers looked for genes shared between ME/CFS and other conditions, and tested whether some blood markers might actually cause ME/CFS risk rather than just being correlated with it. They found that having naturally more mitochondrial DNA (the energy-factory part of cells) seemed protective, while two markers of inflammation and blood-clotting activity seemed to raise risk.

## Why this matters

This is genetic evidence — not just an association in sick patients — pointing to cellular energy production and low-grade inflammation/clotting as likely causes rather than side effects of ME/CFS, which supports the biological rationale behind drugs targeting mitochondria or clotting/inflammation.
