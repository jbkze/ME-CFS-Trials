## Summary

Researchers combined two separate sets of ME/CFS patients' gene-activity data to find a shared pattern of 46 genes that behave differently in ME/CFS, then used a computer search to find existing drugs that might reverse that pattern. Three of the four candidate drugs — including pioglitazone, an already-approved diabetes medicine — gave a modest boost to energy production when tested on muscle cells grown in the lab.

## Why this matters

Flags specific already-approved drugs that target how cells produce energy, which could be tested in ME/CFS patients faster than developing a brand-new drug, and adds independent computational support to the energy-production theory of ME/CFS.
