## Summary

In a rat study, injecting a piece of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein into the brain fluid made the animals voluntarily exercise less (a stand-in for fatigue), while letting them exercise on their own reduced the brain inflammation that the spike protein had caused.

_(Full text in `paper.txt`; the summary above is the curated plain-language abstract — deepen from the full text when reading.)_

## Why this matters

An animal model directly linking a coronavirus protein to brain inflammation and reduced activity supports the idea that leftover viral material can trigger the kind of brain inflammation thought to drive post-viral fatigue, and hints that some exercise may calm rather than worsen it in this model — a nuance worth watching against reports that exertion harms ME/CFS patients.
