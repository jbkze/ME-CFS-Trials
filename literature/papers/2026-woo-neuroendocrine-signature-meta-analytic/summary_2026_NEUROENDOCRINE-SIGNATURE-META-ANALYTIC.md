# Neuroendocrine signature of ME/CFS: Meta-analytic evidence for bioactive cortisol deficit and exaggerated feedback sensitivity

## Summary

The body's main stress hormone, cortisol, is made and controlled by a chain of glands called the HPA axis (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis). Pooling 46 earlier studies (about 1,400 ME/CFS patients vs 1,350 healthy people), researchers found ME/CFS patients consistently have lower cortisol in their saliva, urine and hair, respond more weakly when given a hormone that should trigger a cortisol surge, and suppress their cortisol too strongly when given a steroid that should dampen it slightly.

## Why this matters

Points to a genuinely blunted, over-braked stress-hormone system in ME/CFS — not just 'stress' in the everyday sense — that could help explain the poor sleep, brain fog, and low resilience to physical or emotional strain patients report, and gives researchers a measurable target (cortisol testing) to explore for future treatments.
