## Summary

Researchers measured autoantibodies — antibodies that mistakenly attack the body's own receptors for stress hormones and nerve signals — in the blood of 96 people with post-COVID condition, 59 with ME/CFS, and 36 healthy people. People with ME/CFS had higher levels of one such antibody (targeting a stress-hormone receptor called beta-2-adrenergic), and higher antibody levels went along with worse autonomic-nervous-system problems (like poor control of blood pressure and heart rate) and slower thinking speed.

## Why this matters

Adds another data point to the idea that misdirected antibodies attacking the body's own receptors help drive ME/CFS symptoms — the same idea behind Scheibenbogen's immunoadsorption trials (IMPACT, IA-Surv) that filter these antibodies out of the blood, though this is a different, Spanish (Basque Country) research group, not Wirth or Scheibenbogen themselves.
