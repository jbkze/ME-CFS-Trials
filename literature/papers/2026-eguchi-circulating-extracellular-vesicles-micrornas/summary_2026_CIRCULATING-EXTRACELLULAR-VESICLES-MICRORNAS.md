## Summary

Researchers compared tiny particles that cells release into the blood (extracellular vesicles) and the small RNA snippets they carry (microRNAs) in people with ME/CFS, people with ordinary chronic fatigue, and people with depression. ME/CFS patients had a distinct pattern of these particles and about 62 microRNAs that stood out compared to the other groups, pointing to disrupted nerve-cell and cell-structure pathways.

## Why this matters

Adds to a growing set of blood-based candidate markers that could eventually help doctors tell ME/CFS apart from look-alike conditions like depression or general tiredness, something there is currently no reliable test for — though this is a small, early exploratory study that needs replication in larger groups.
