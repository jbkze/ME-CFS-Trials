# Impact of immunoadsorption on autoantibodies and physical performance in post-COVID patients: a prospective exploratory study of 18 participants

## Related files
- [[literature/papers/2026-wossidlo-impact-immunoadsorption-autoantibodies-physical/citation.bib]]

## Summary

Immunoadsorption is a treatment that filters antibodies out of the blood. In 18 post-COVID patients with raised levels of antibodies that mistakenly target the body's own stress-hormone receptors, the treatment clearly lowered those antibody levels — but the drop faded back within 3-6 months, and it did not improve how well patients performed on objective physical tests, only slightly easing self-reported pain and fatigue.

## Why this matters

A cautionary, negative-leaning result — from a different German university group, not Charité — on the same 'filter out the bad antibodies' approach behind Scheibenbogen's immunoadsorption trials, useful context for interpreting whether removing these antibodies alone is enough to relieve symptoms.
