## Summary

Klaus Wirth argues that people with ME/CFS often have too little fluid in their blood vessels (hypovolemia), caused by broken salt-and-water retention signals in the body, leaky small blood vessels, and starved muscle cells pulling fluid out of the blood during activity.

## Why this matters

From Wirth (Mitodicure): ties together low blood volume, dizziness on standing, and the post-exertional crash (a crash after activity) into one physical explanation, reinforcing the case for his group's muscle/circulation-focused treatment approach — but it's an early, not-yet-peer-reviewed hypothesis paper, so treat it as a working idea pending review.
