# Targeting Bioenergetic, Redox and Prostaglandin Pathways in Long COVID-Associated Post-Exertional Malaise and Brain Fog: A Nutraceutical Translational Hypothesis

## Related files
- [[literature/papers/2026-praet-targeting-bioenergetic-redox-prostaglandin/citation.bib]]

## Summary

This hypothesis paper proposes that the post-exertional crash (PEM) and brain fog in long COVID come from three overlapping problems: poorly working cellular energy factories (mitochondria), an imbalance in the body's antioxidant defenses, and ongoing inflammation involving a molecule called prostaglandin E2. The author suggests combining eight existing supplements (including CoQ10, selenium, and fish oil) that each target one of these three problems, though the combination itself has not yet been tested in patients.

## Why this matters

Offers a mechanism-based rationale for a low-risk supplement combination targeting post-exertional malaise (a crash after activity) and brain fog — in the same spirit as other mitochondrial/redox theories already in this database — but it is an untested, single-author hypothesis; the author himself says direct evidence is 'limited to very limited,' so treat it as an idea awaiting a real trial, not a proven treatment.

## Notes

Posted Preprints.org 2026-07-14 (v1), not yet peer-reviewed. Single-author narrative hypothesis/translational-rationale paper (no new experimental data); proposes a future stratified RCT to test a 3-module nutraceutical combination (bioenergetic: CoQ10 + alpha-lipoic acid; redox: selenium + sulforaphane + resveratrol; prostaglandin: Boswellia serrata + luteolin + EPA). Focuses on Long COVID PEM/brain fog broadly (ME/CFS-overlapping population), not ME/CFS-exclusively.
