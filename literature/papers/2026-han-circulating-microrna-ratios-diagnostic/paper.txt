Abstract
Background Long COVID is a multisystemic condition with heterogeneous clinical
presentations lacking diagnostic biomarkers with discriminatory capacity. This study
screened circulating microRNAs as candidate biomarkers for long COVID detection by
miRNA-sequencing, followed by targeted qPCR quantiﬁcation focusing on miRNA ratios to
reduce technical variability.
Methods Overall, 206 long COVID patients ( > 12 weeks after acute infection) and 71
uninfected, unvaccinated healthy controls were recruited through our prospective
POSTCOV registry. Candidate microRNAs were screened by microRNA sequencing and
quantiﬁed by targeted quantitative polymerase chain reaction, focusing on microRNA ratios
expressed as cycle-threshold differences. In a training cohort, we developed a logistic
regression model based on two microRNA ratios with age and sex as covariates. Internal
validation was evaluated using cross validation and bootstrap. We then test the model in an
independent external validation cohort processed with the same normalization and ratio
deﬁnition.
Results Here weshow both microRNA ratios (hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p, hsa-miR-345-
5p/hsa-miR-223-3p) are inversely associated with long COVID. Discrimination in the training
cohort is moderate (AUC: 0.760), with acceptable calibration (intercept ~0, slope ~1, Brier
score 0.131). In external validation, discrimination decreases (AUC: 0.676) and calibration
reveals systematic risk overestimation (intercept −1.88, slope 0.77). Nevertheless, both
ratios are consistently lower in long COVID compared to controls.
Conclusion Consistent between-group ratio differences support these microRNAs as
candidate biomarkers, whereas limited external model performance highlights the
challenges of transportability in heterogeneous long COVID cohorts. Target enrichment
indicates inﬂammatory signaling pathways and integration with drug perturbation proﬁles
suggests compounds for future validation and repurposing studies.

Plain Language Summary
Long COVID is a serious condition resulting
from SARS-CoV-2 infection. It is associated
with persistent symptoms affecting multiple
organ systems, yet objective blood-based

technical differences. These ﬁndings support
circulating microRNAs as a promising signal

dation in larger, well-matched populations is
required before clinical implementation.

disease course, therapeutic options, and prognostic outlook3. The general
consensus regarding the deﬁnition of long COVID is the persistent burden
of symptoms at 12 weeks after a SARS-CoV-2 infection, irrespective of
whether individuals failed to recover from their initial symptoms or
experienced newly emerging symptoms following an apparent convalescent
period. Diagnosis of long COVID is mainly achieved by the process of
elimination of other illnesses that may cause similar symptoms4. However,
due to the heterogenous presentation forms and different clusters of
symptoms, this approach requires generous time and resources. Therefore,
the lack of diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for long COVID is
an ongoing research gap requiring further studies5. For some LC pre-
sentationtypes(mainlyneurologicalorpulmonarysymptoms),interleukin-

were proposed as such blood biomarkers6.
MicroRNAs (miRNAs), short single-stranded non-coding RNA
molecules usually around 17 to 22 nucleotides long, are stably expressed in
the blood and could be used as liquid biopsies7. This makes them interesting
as potential diagnostic biomarkers due to their involvement in many reg-
ulatory and structural housekeeping pathways8. Moreover, miRNA ratios
frombothtissueand circulatingmiRNAs have beendeveloped as diagnostic
tools for discriminating melanoma from nevi or breast cancer screening9,10.
In chronic heart failure, selected miRNAs have been extensively studied as
biomarkerstopredictfuturehospitalisation11.InacuteCOVID-19infection,
some miRNAs have been found to be associated with disease severity and
mortality in hospitalized COVID-19 patients12. Other studies have also
implicated potential miRNAs for their role in the pathomechanisms of pain
symptoms in long COVID13.
Beyond the potential as a biomarker, miRNAs may also offer
mechanistic insights into disease processesand epigenetic regulation, which
can be useful for informing therapeutic strategies by revealing candidate
compounds that modulate relevant pathways. Lately, advances in tran-
scriptomics and machine learning have enabled drug repurposing efforts by
matching disease-related gene expression signatures with known pharma-
cological proﬁles, thus accelerating the identiﬁcation of therapeutic leads
from existing compounds14. As of yet, no speciﬁc drug has been approved
for the treatment of long COVID, due to the lack of knowledge on the exact
disease mechanisms. Given the hypothesized role of persistent endothelial
dysfunction, coagulation abnormalities and prolonged inﬂammation in
long COVID, antiviral, immune modulation, anticoagulation, antiplatelet
and inhibition of neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation have been
proposed as possible therapies15. Hence, the need for evidence-based ther-
apeutic options besides supportive care and cardiopulmonary rehabilitation
remainspertinent,particularlyforworking-ageadultswhosequalityoflifeis
gravely affected16; in this context, the integration of transcriptomics-
informed drug repurposing into evolving treatment frameworks offers a
promising outlook to lessen long COVID symptom burden.
Thisstudyaims toidentifydifferentiallyexpressed circulatingmiRNAs
in long COVID patients with predominant cardiovascular, pulmonary, or
neurological symptoms using a two-step approach consisting of miRNA-
seq followed by RT-qPCR and additional external validation of the con-
structed miRNA ratio prediction model with a test cohort. Across cohorts,
the key miRNA ratios remain directionally consistent betweenlong COVID
and controls, supporting their potential as reproducible biomarker signals.
Predicted mRNA targets of the prioritized miRNAs implicate pathways
related to immune signaling, vascular biology and cellular stress responses,
which provides mechanistic hypotheses for long COVID pathophysiology.
Methods
Study population
This study was conducted in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration and
with approval of the local ethics review board of the Medical University of
Vienna (EC registration number: 1008/2021 approval date: 24 February
2021, and EC registration number: 1758/2022 approval date: 11 November
2022, ClinicalTrials.gov Identiﬁer: NCT05398952). Study participants were
recruited consecutively and gave their written informed consent. Long
COVID patients were prospectively enrolled in the Vienna PostCOV
Registry between March 2021 and April 2023 from our specialized long
COVID outpatient clinic,while the healthy controls were recruited between
May 2020 and June 2020, who were all COVID-naive and non-vaccinated
(EC registration number: 1387/2020 approval date: 20 April 2020).
Adult patients with long COVID symptoms after a deﬁnitive infection
with SARS-CoV-2(conﬁrmed by PCR test),aftera minimum of 12 weeksof
acute infection, were included in this study and further characterized into
three subgroups according to their main symptom burden: cardiovascular
(Cardio), pulmonary (Pulmo) and neurological/neuropsychiatric (Neuro)
(Study ﬂowchart: Fig. 1). These symptom groups were formed based on
reports from existing literature and in accordance with the core outcome set
for post-COVID-19 condition of the international Delphi consensus
study17,18. Long COVID patients with chest pain, palpitations, abnormalities
in left ventricular or right ventricular pump function, chronic pericarditis/
myocarditis proven by cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, or new heart
rhythm disorders without respiratory abnormalities (normal chest X-ray,
lungfunctionandchestcomputertomography)wereclassiﬁedastheCardio
group. Pulmo group patients were described as having resting or exertional
dyspnea with pathological lung function or residual opacity in chest CT,
without cardiac manifestation of long COVID syndrome. Patients with no
objective organ disease pertaining to heart or lung were characterized into
the Neuro group, if their main symptoms involved fatigue, headaches,
concentration difﬁculties, vegetative symptoms and paresthesia. Exclusion
criteria were non-willingness to participate or withdrawal of informed
consent, participation in any SARS-CoV-2 pharmacological trial, preg-
nancy, renal insufﬁciency (glomerular ﬁltration rate <45 ml/min/1.73 m2)
or systemic inﬂammatory disease.
Clinical parameters
As part of the standardized workup of long COVID patients at our out-
patient clinic specialized for post-COVID conditions at the Department of
Internal Medicine II, Division of Cardiology, of the Medical University of
Vienna, type and severity of long COVID symptoms, date of COVID-19
infection(s), medical history and comorbidities, vital parameters, current
medication and COVID vaccinations were recorded. Clinical ﬁndings and
reports of blood and imaging tests performed prior to their baseline visit
(such as lab work, spirometry, chest X-ray, cardiac MRI) were recorded, as
well as the results of a venous blood draw and electrocardiogram (ECG) on
the day of the baseline visit. Quality of life was assessed with the EQ-5D-3L
questionnaire in German, including a visual analogue scale (VAS) for
general quality of life (range 0 /worst/–100 /best/)19.
Sample collection and processing
Serum and EDTA-plasma samples were obtained through peripheral
venipuncture at baseline and follow-up visits. EDTA-plasma samples were
processed immediately by centrifugation and frozen at −80 °C until further
analysis at the central biobank of the Medical University of Vienna. Serum
samples were left standing to allow for clotting for 15 min before cen-
trifugation for 15 min at 1500 g at 4 °C and then frozen at −80 °C until
further analysis at the central biobank of the Medical University of Vienna.
Blood count, coagulation, electrolytes, kidney function, liver function, car-
diac enzymes and inﬂammatory parameters were processed and measured
by standard routine protocols at the diagnostic laboratory of the Depart-
ment of Laboratory Medicine, Medical University of Vienna. Standard
routine diagnostic tests were conducted on a cobas e801 analyzer (Roche) at
the diagnostic laboratory of the Department of Laboratory Medicine,
Medical University of Vienna, to measure total protein, albumin, ferritin,
CRP, high-density lipoproteins (HDL) and low-density lipoproteins (LDL).

RSC48 instrument. Samples were centrifuged for 5 minutes at 12000 g at
4 °C to remove cellular debris. For each sample, 200 µL sample or 100 µL

RNA sequencing and library preparation
Sequencing was performed in SR100 mode on Illumina’s NovaSeq 6000
device using the SP ﬂowcell. Overall quality of the next-generation
sequencing (NGS) data was evaluated automatically and manually with
fastQC v0.11.9 and multiQC v1.1020,21. Reads from all passing samples were
adapter trimmed and quality ﬁltered using cutadapt v3.3 and ﬁltered for a
minimum length of 17nt22. Mapping steps were performed with bowtie
v1.3.023 and miRDeep2 v2.0.1.224, whereas reads were mapped ﬁrst against
thegenomicreferenceGRCh38.p12providedbyEnsembl25allowingfortwo
mismatches, and subsequently26, ﬁltered for miRNAsof hsa (Homo sapiens)
only, allowing for one mismatch. Statistical analysis of pre-processed NGS
data was done with R v4.027.
Analysis of differentially expressed microRNAs
Atstudydesign,nodatawereavailableregardingmiRNAsassociatedtolong
COVID disease, so the sample size and power calculation could only be
estimated based on existing miRNA literature data in other diseases28. The
discovery sequencing phase was therefore planned as an exploratory screen
informed by prior small RNA-seq studies and intended to nominate can-
didates for downstream veriﬁcation. With 20 participants per group, a
comparison at two-sided α = 0.05 provides approximately 80% power to
detect a standardized mean difference of Cohen’s d ≈0.91, which corre-
sponds to an expression difference of about ~1.5-fold change. Differential
expression in the discovery phase was assessed using false discovery rate
(FDR) control at 0.2 for a balanceof type I and type II error rates. Candidate
miRNAs taken forward to RT-qPCR were selected based on a combination
of (i) FDR-signiﬁcant differential expression and effect size ranking in the
discovery phase and (ii) a priori biological or clinical relevance supported by
published evidence,evenifnotmeetingtheFDRthreshold indiscovery.The
validation phase was therefore considered conﬁrmatory for the selected
candidates. Differential expression (DE) analysis with edgeR v3.32 used the
quasi-likelihood negative binomial generalized log-linear model functions
providedbythepackage29.TheindependentﬁlteringmethodofDESeq2was
adapted for use with edgeR to remove low abundant miRNAs and thus
optimize the FDR correction30. A logarithmic fold change (logFC) of ≥| 0.5|
and a FDR of < 0.05 were considered statistically signiﬁcant for sequenced
miRNAs for subsequent mRNA target prediction. Heatmaps were gener-
ated with pheatmap (version 1.0.12).
Target prediction
MiRTarBase (Release 9.0)31 and Target Scan (http://www.targetscan.org/
vert_80)32 were used to predict the mRNA targets of the differentially
expressed miRNAs. Targets were included for further analysis if they were
validated by a strong evidence method in miRTarBase or if they had a
cumulative weighted context + + score of ≤−0.5 in Target Scan.
For visualization, Cytoscape (version 3.9.1)33 was used to graph the
network.
Gene set enrichment analysis
We used the web server Enrichr (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/, last
accessed on 28 March 2025) to perform pathway enrichment analysis by
inputting the genes from target prediction, consolidating the Reactome,
MSigDBHallmarkandWikiPathwaysdatabases.Thetoptenpathwayswith
an adjusted p-value < 0.05 and counts > 2 were visualised34–36. The protein-
protein interaction (PPI) network analysis was performed using STRING
(https://string-db.org/, last accessed on 03 Jan 2025)37.

MiRNA ratios, random forest analysis and logistic
regression model
MiRNA ratios (ΔCt) were formed for small RNA-seq and RT-qPCR
samples and compared with the Mann-Whitney U test with adjustment
for multiple testing using Benjamini-Hochberg FDR between long
COVID and healthy groups. The miRNA ratios dataset was initially
assessed for missing values, with columns containing more than 10%
missing data removed. The remaining missing values were imputed using
median imputation, due to skewed distributions of the miRNA ratios.
Weighted random forest analyses were performed (R package random-
Forest, version 4.7-1.2) with standard settings and 1000 trees to identify
the most important miRNA ratios in the classiﬁcation of patients with
long COVID compared to those without long COVID39. Feature
importance was assessed using the mean decrease in Gini impurity. The
model’s predictive performance was evaluated using the area under the
receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC).
Logistic regression models were assessed using miRNA ratios as pre-
dictors. The ﬁnal logistic regression model included the top two important
miRNA ratios (hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p and hsa-miR-345-5p/ hsa-
miR-223-3p) along with age and sex as covariates. Odds ratios (OR) were
reported per 1 Ct increase with 95% conﬁdence intervals. The Variance
Inﬂation Factor (VIF) was <3 for all variables in the regression models,
indicating minimal multicollinearity. Model ﬁt was summarized using the
Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), and nested model comparisons were
performed using likelihood-ratio tests (R package caret, version 6.0-94).
DiscriminationwasassessedusingROCcurveanalysisandAUC(Rpackage
pROC, version 1.18.5). For descriptive classiﬁcation, a confusion matrix was
generated to assess sensitivity, speciﬁcity, likelihood ratios, positive pre-
dictive value, negative predictive value and accuracy at the threshold of 0.80.
Internal validation of the ﬁnal model was performed using 5-fold cross-
validation and nonparametric bootstrap resampling (R = 1000) (R package
boot, version 1.3-28.1). Calibration was assessed using calibration-in-the-
large (CITL/intercept), calibration slope, and Brier score. Calibration was
additionally visualized by grouping predictions into deciles of predicted risk
and comparing mean predicted probabilities to observed event rates.
A separate group of 96 long COVID patients and 31 healthy controls
were included as an independent validation cohort (test group). External
validation was performed by applying the ﬁnal logistic regression model,
constructed on the RT-qPCR miRNA values, to the test group without
reﬁtting. Discrimination was quantiﬁed by AUC with bootstrap 95% con-
ﬁdenceintervals.CalibrationwasassessedusingCITL,calibrationslope,and
Brier score, supported by a calibration plot (R package rms, version 6.7-1).
Logistic recalibration by updating the intercept and slope of the original
linear predictor was calculated in the validation cohort as a sensitivity
analysis.
Identiﬁcation of therapeutic targets by drug signature enrich-
ment analysis
To identify candidate compounds capable of reversing the gene expression
signature regulated by differentially expressed miRNAs, we used the LINCS
L1000ChemicalPerturbagenConsensusSignatures(ChemPert) datasetvia
the Enrichr platform (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/)40,41. The input
gene set comprised upregulated target genes inferred from differentially
expressed miRNAs from the validation cohort. Compounds were ranked
based on their adjusted p-values and combined enrichment scores.
Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were performed with R (version 2022.12.0 + 353). Data
are presented as mean ± standard deviation (normal distribution) or med-
ian (interquartile range, IQR). The normal distribution assumption was
testedwiththeShapiro-Wilkstest.One-wayANOVAofmeanswasusedfor
post-hoc analysis comparing differences between groups. The Benjamini-
Hochberg method was applied to correct for multiple testing ( = false dis-
covery rate, FDR). A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically
signiﬁcant.
Results
Patient characteristics
Overall, the median age of the 110 long COVID patients (71% female) and
40 healthy controls (65% female) was 40.5 and 50 years, respectively. The
patient cohort consisted mainly of non-smokers (91.5%), and most had a
mild course of COVID-19, not requiring hospitalisation (92.7%). The
median time after COVID-19 infection was 244 days. Seventy-four patients
(67.3%) had received a COVID vaccination before the blood draw at our
specialized long COVID clinic. Long COVID patients reported a moderate
decrease in general quality of life with a median VAS score of 40 out of 100
(quality of life scale: 0 = worst, 100 = best), while healthy controls stated a
median of 85.5 out of 100.
For the ﬁrst step of biomarker discovery by small RNA-seq, we
included samples of 20 LC patients and 20 controls, while as a second step
the other 90 LC samples and 20 controls were used for validation. Supple-
mentary Table S1 and Supplementary Table S2 provide detailed descriptive
characteristics of the long COVID patients (n = 20) and healthy controls
(n = 20) for discovery and a separate independent validation cohort (90 LC
and 19 healthy controls).The general distribution of clinical parameterswas
similar between patients of the small RNA-seq and the RT-qPCR cohort.

Validation cohort via miRNA RT-qPCR
For a comprehensive study of miRNAs in long COVID patients, an addi-
tional 90 serum samples from an independent (not previously analyzed)
long COVID cohort were subjected to measurement of miRNA expression
with RT-qPCR, along with 20 healthy controls. MiRNAs were selected due
toeithertheirpresenceinmiRNA-seqasdifferentiallyexpressedorbasedon
previously reported interesting aspects found in the literature (Suppl.
Table S5). Due to the hemolysis index of above 7, ﬁve samples from the long
COVID cohort and one control sample were excluded for further analysis.
Three miRNAsweresigniﬁcantly(adj.p < 0.05) downregulated inlong
COVID patients by RT-qPCR measurement: hsa-miR-210-3p, hsa-miR-
345-5p, and hsa-miR-484 (Fig. 3A), while hsa-miR-9-5p was upregulated in
the LC group compared to controls (Table 1). In subgroup analyses (Suppl.
Figs.S1–S3),theoverallpatternoflowermiRNAabundanceinlongCOVID
was preserved across cardiovascular, neurologic. and pulmonary pheno-
types. Compared with controls, the Cardio subgroup showed signiﬁcantly
lower levels of hsa-miR-210-3p (p < 0.01), hsa-miR-345-5p (p = 0.04) and
hsa-miR-484 (p = 0.03), alongside a modest increase in hsa-miR-122-5p
(p = 0.02) (Fig. S1). The Neuro subgroup likewise displayed reduced hsa-
miR-210-3p (p = 0.02) and hsa-miR-484 (p = 0.03) relative to controls
(Fig. S2). In the pulmonary subgroup, hsa-miR-345-5p (p = 0.01) and hsa-
miR-484 (p = 0.02) remained lower than in controls, while hsa-miR-9-5p
was higher (p < 0.01) (Figure S3).
MiRNA ratios for predicting long COVID and internal model
validation
MiRNA ratios were constructed separately for RNA-seq and RT-qPCR
cohorts. 208 miRNA ratios were signiﬁcantly different between long
COVID and healthy individuals in the RNA-seq cohort, while only two
miRNA ratios were for the RT-qPCR group (Fig. 3B). The two miRNA
ratios were miR-484/miR-126-3p and miR-126-3p/ miR-484. Using a
random forest classiﬁer the model classiﬁed long COVID cases with very
low class error with a classiﬁcation error of 1.18%, but performed poorly for
controls with 78.95% of the healthy controls as false positives (Fig. 3C). The
random forest prediction model revealed miR-484/miR-126-3p and miR-
345-5p/miR-233-3p as the top two most important variables (Fig. 3D) and
had an AUC of 0.714 (95% CI: 0.585-0.842, DeLong)(Fig. 3E). The out-of-
bag estimate of error rate was 15.38%.
Logistic regression model development and internal validation were
conducted in the training cohort (n = 104; LC prevalence 0.817). The AUC
for predicting long COVID with hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p, hsa-miR-
345-5p/hsa-miR-223-3p and hsa-miR-210-3p/hsa-miR-126-3p by them-
selves was 0.77, 0.69 and 0.71, respectively (Fig. S4). Model 1 included the
top twomiRNAratioshsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3pandhsa-miR-345-5p/
hsa-miR-223-3p from the random forest analysis (AIC: 94.14), while Model
2used thetop twomiRNAratioswithageandsexascovariates(AIC:94.86).
Due to the small sample size and to avoid overﬁtting, cross-validation and
bootstrap analyses were performed. In Model 1, both miRNAratios showed
inverse directions with LC (Table 2). Higher Ct values of hsa-miR-484/hsa-
miR-126-3p were directionally inverse to the odds of long COVID
(OR = 0.516, 95% CI 0.214–1.162), and likewise for hsa-miR-345-5p/hsa-
miR-223-3p (OR = 0.600, 95% CI; 0.295–1.147). Discrimination was
moderate with an AUC = 0.752, and sensitivity was 70.6% and speciﬁcity
68.4% (Fig. 4A).
In Model 2, after adjustment for demographic covariates, both miRNA
predictors remained directionally inverse with LC: OR 0.487 (95% CI:
0.200–1.102) for hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p and OR 0.619 (95% CI:
0.298–1.210) for hsa-miR-345-5p/hsa-miR-223-3p. Age showed OR 0.964

External validation and sensitivity analysis
External validation was performed in an independent cohort (n = 127; LC
n = 96, Control n = 31), which had comparable characteristics to the initial
RT-qPCR cohort. Detailedbaseline characteristics for this additional cohort
are depicted in Suppl. Table S6. Model coefﬁcients and decision threshold
were ﬁxed on the training cohort prior to evaluation in the external vali-
dation cohort. On external validation, discrimination was moderate
(AUC = 0.676; bootstrap 95% CI: 0.564 to 0.792) (Fig. 4D). Calibration
assessment showed systematic overprediction (CITL −1.74, 95% CI: −2.16
to −1.28) and a calibration slope below 1 (0.774; 95% CI: 0.276–1.318). The
Brier score was 0.212. Predicted probabilities were generally high (decile
mean predicted risk approximately 0.83–0.99), and at a probability cutoff of
0.80, sensitivity was 97.9% and speciﬁcity 3.2% (TP 94, FN 2; TN 1, FP 30),
indicating limited transportability of the absolute probability scale of the
logistic regression model to separate cohorts. As an exploratory analysis, the
ROC-derivedbestthresholdinthevalidationcohortwasassessedandwould
be 0.957 with a sensitivity of 63.5% and speciﬁcity of 74.2%.
Logistic recalibration of the linear predictor yielded a negative recali-
bration intercept ( −1.225) and a recalibration slope < 1 (0.774), consistent
with overconﬁdent predictions. Recalibration improved overall probability
accuracy as reﬂected by a lower Brierscore of 0.171. Calibration plots can be
found in Suppl. Fig. S5.
Nevertheless, both miRNA ratios that deﬁne the model showed a
consistent directional shift between long COVID and controls across
cohorts. For hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p, values were lower in long
COVID than in controls in the training cohort (mean −3.231 vs. −2.651)
and this separation persisted in the test cohort (mean −4.349 vs. −4.009)
with comparable standard deviations (Fig. 3E). Likewise, for hsa-miR-345-
5p/hsa-miR-223-3p, long COVID showed lower values than controls in
training (mean −8.857 vs. −8.148) and in the test cohort (mean −9.811 vs.
−9.097) (Fig. 3F). Median and interquartile ranges followed the same pat-
tern for both ratios, indicating that the shift between the cohorts was not
driven by a small number of extreme values.

Discussion
Inourcase-controlstudyfromtheViennaPOSTCOVregistry,weidentiﬁed
several differentially expressed circulating miRNAs between long COVID
patients and healthy controls. Target prediction and pathway enrichment
analyses showed enriched genes involved in cell signaling, inﬂammation,
angiogenesis, and T-cell regulation. In an independent RT-qPCR cohort,
three miRNAs (hsa-miR-210-3p, hsa-miR-345-5p, hsa-miR-484) remained
signiﬁcantly lower in long COVID, while hsa-miR-9-5p was higher. Sub-
group analyses suggested that the overall pattern of reduced miRNA
abundance in long COVID was preserved across cardiovascular, neurologic
and pulmonary phenotypes. Further, the miRNA ratio hsa-miR-484/hsa-
miR-126-3pwasidentiﬁedasadiscriminatorbetweenlongCOVIDpatients
and healthy controls in both the RNA-seq and the RT-qPCR cohort with
consistently lower ratio values in long COVID patients compared to con-
trols. We constructed a logistic regression model using hsa-miR-484/hsa-
miR-126-3p and hsa-miR-345-5p/hsa-miR-223-3p for predicting long
COVID and validated this model on an independent cohort, which showed
moderate discrimination (AUC: 0.676), but some batch effect despite the
same normalization procedures as to the training cohort.
Several of the miRNAs from our analysis, including hsa-miR-484, hsa-
miR-345-5p, hsa-miR-210-3p, hsa-miR-144-5p, hsa-miR-199a-5p, hsa-
miR-233-3p, and hsa-miR-126-3p, have been previously reported as reg-
ulators of immune function, inﬂammation, hypoxia, and vascular
dysfunction42–46. Notably, miR-484 has been associated with mitochondrial
stress by targeting FIS1, a key mediator of mitochondrial ﬁssion. This
interactioncaninﬂuencecellularapoptosisandhasbeenobservedinvarious
cell types, including cardiomyocytes and adrenocortical cancer cells47. Inthe
context of COVID-19, mitochondrial dysfunction and endothelial damage
are prominent features, suggesting that dysregulation of miR-484 may
contribute to these pathologies. We have previously published our ﬁndings
of higher levels of autoantibodies against endothelin type A receptor
(ETAR) in long COVID patients with orthostatic intolerance correlating to
higher symptom burden, additionally supporting the hypothesis that
endothelial dysfunction could play a role in the pathophysiology of long
COVID48.

The most discriminative variable from our analyses was hsa-miR-484/
hsa-miR-126-3p, which showed moderate diagnostic accuracy for long
COVID with an AUC of 0.676 in external validation analysis. This miRNA
ratio integrates two biologically meaningful signals and may reﬂect an
imbalance between mitochondrial stress signaling and endothelial repair
mechanisms: hsa-miR-484 has been reported in the context of mitochon-
drialand endothelialdysfunction, whilehsa-miR-126-3p playsa centralrole
in angiogenesis and vascular inﬂammation49. In our dataset, a lower ratio
was strongly associated with long COVID. This could suggest either
downregulation of hsa-miR-484 in response to apoptotic stimulation or a
relative increase in hsa-miR-126-3p expression, potentially a compensatory
response to endothelial injury or dysfunction in patients with persistent
symptoms after acute COVID-19 infection. This would be in line with a
recent pediatric study that reported a distinct protein signature from blood
plasma in children, which was characterized by ongoing inﬂammatory and
pro-angiogenic signals50. These discoveries suggest that the miRNA ratio
may reﬂect underlying pathophysiological mechanisms relevant to
long COVID.
At a set classiﬁcation threshold of 0.8, our logistic regression model
with hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p, hsa-miR-345-5p/hsa-miR-223-3p and
age and sex as covariates did not transfer well to the external validation
cohort. However, despite reduction of AUC (from 0.760 to 0.676), the
miRNA ratios showed consistent directionality for long COVID patients
compared to controls (Fig. 4E, Fig. 4F). Performance metrics of our model
remain similar to the AUC values reported by Szögi et al., ranging from
0.715 to 0.758, in their investigation of proposing the use of circulating cell-
freemitochondrial DNAin cerebral spinal ﬂuid as a new biomarkerfor long
COVID51. Notably, the distributions for both ratios were shifted toward
more negative values in the external cohort for both controls and long
COVID (e.g., miR-484/miR-126-3p control median −4.14 in external vs.
−2.77 in training, miR-345/miR-223 control median −9.14 vs. −8.32),
consistent with a cohort effect on the measurement scale. This systematic
shift provides a plausible explanation for the calibration intercept < 0 and
the very high predicted probabilities in validation when applying ﬁxed
coefﬁcients and the training-derived cutoff. However, the preserved direc-
tionality of lower miRNA ratios in long COVID consistent across cohorts
suggeststheseratiosmaystillcapturea biologicallyrelevantsignalandcould
remain useful as relative biomarkers, provided that the model may be
transferred through better methods of recalibration, batch harmonization,
or cohort-speciﬁc thresholding before clinical application.
Past studies have mainly evaluated miRNAs for their prognostic value
in hospitalized COVID-19 patients during the acute infection phase. In this
setting, using miRNA ratios brings the advantage of technical variability
stabilization compared to singular miRNA values, while integrating corre-
lated biology. Molinero et al. found the miR-199a-5p/miR-9-5p ratio from
bronchial aspirate samples the most accurate for risk stratiﬁcation for cri-
tically ill COVID-19 patients after initiation of invasive mechanical venti-
lation to predict intensive care unit (ICU) mortality52. In another
exploratory study of hospitalized COVID patients, more severe courses of
disease, deﬁned as ICU stay, were linked to higher miR-223-3p and miR-
126-3p levels53. In our long COVID cohorts, both miR-126-3p and miR-
223-3p likewise emerged as recurring components of the most informative
ratios (hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p and hsa-miR-345-5p/hsa-miR-223-
3p), supporting their broader relevance across the COVID-19 disease
spectrum. Although long COVID is clinically distinct from acute severe
COVID-19, the repeated involvement of endothelial- and myeloid-
associated miRNAs is consistent with hypotheses implicating persistent
immune activation and vascular dysregulation in post-acute disease. In this
context, considering that persistent viral RNA orprotein antigens have been
hypothesized as pathomechanisms of long COVID, anti-inﬂammatory and
antiviral propertiesof drug compounds may be of particular interest, for the
low-grade inﬂammation disease state leading to ongoing symptoms54.

Exploratory drug target analysis showed some compounds were pre-
dicted to reverse the miRNA-mRNA signature, such as 5-iodotubercidin,
secalciferol or ZG-10 (c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) inhibitor).
5-Iodotubercidin is a selective adenosine kinase (ADK) inhibitor that has
been shown to attenuate endothelial activation in inﬂammatory settings by
suppressing TNF-α–induced adhesion molecule expression58. In addition,
5-Iodotubercidin has been reported to inhibit SARS-CoV-2 RNA synthesis
ina cell-based RNAreplicase reportersystem,withtheantiviraleffectlinked
to ADK activity rather than direct polymerase binding59. Secalciferol is a
vitamin D3 metabolite; vitamin D metabolites in general have been attrib-
uted with immunomodulatory and anti-inﬂammatory effects across
immune cells, providing a plausible rationale for its appearance among
connectivity hits60. ZG-10, a c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) inhibitor, can
target JNK, which has a role in inﬂammatory signaling and was shown to
support coronavirus replication cycles61. Still, most compounds highlighted
by connectivity mapping remain preclinical and should only be viewed as
starting points for mechanistic validation rather than candidates for
immediate off-label use. In the real-world setting, a large patient-reported
treatment survey with 1800 long COVID patients reportedon the perceived
beneﬁt from several off-label symptomatic therapies, such as using IVIG/
IgG, antihistamines, beta blockers, or pyridostigmine62. Reported responses
varied across symptom-deﬁned subgroups, underscoring that off-label
management is best individualized rather than applied uniformly62.
This study has some limitations. First, the cohort was recruited from a
single specialized outpatient clinic and included only adults, which may
limit generalizability to broader populations, including pediatric patients
with long COVID. The control design did not include time-matched post
infection controls, so some differences may reﬂect the post infection status
rather than long COVID-speciﬁc biology. Although subgroup classiﬁcation
was grounded in objective clinical, imaging, and functional criteria, symp-
tom overlap, and subclinical manifestations may have inﬂuenced group
allocation. RNA-seq and qPCR analyses were restricted to blood-derived
miRNA proﬁles, which may not fully capture tissue-speciﬁc regulatory
processes, and qPCR experiments had to be performed in batches, which
could have introduced batch effects. Further, the single time point design
does not allow for following the longitudinal changes in the identiﬁed
miRNA ratios. Longitudinal studies will therefore be necessary to char-
acterize the temporal dynamics of these miRNA measures and clarify how
the associated miRNA-mRNA-regulated pathways evolve over time. The
sample size, particularly within subgroups, may constrain statistical power.
Nonetheless, stringent measures were implemented throughout, such as the
inclusion of spike-in controls for RNA extraction and hemolysis indices to
minimize pre-analytical variability, rigorous quality control protocols for
next-generation sequencing and RT-qPCR analyses, and target prediction
based on validated databases with conservative thresholds to enhance spe-
ciﬁcity. While these strategies strengthen the robustness of our ﬁndings,
functional validation of miRNA-target interactions and drug target pre-
dictions based on in silico enrichment analyses is still required. Thus, the
ﬁndings of this study should be interpreted with due caution, while
acknowledging the comprehensive and systematic approach applied across
clinical, molecular and statistical domains.
Conclusion
We identiﬁed differentially expressed miRNAs in long COVID patients
following initial mild COVID-19 disease, with hsa-miR-484, hsa-miR-345-
5p and hsa-miR-126-3p being implicated across multiple detection mod-
alities. Despite lower discrimination and miscalibration in external valida-
tion, both miRNA ratios from the model are directionally consistent in
being lower compared to the controls of their cohort. This suggests a
reproducible association with long COVID, even if systematic shifts may
still be present depending on batch. Thus, hsa-miR-484/hsa-miR-126-3p
and hsa-miR-345-5p/hsa-miR-223-3p may capture biologically relevant
signal but are sensitive to cohort and pre-analytical differences. Further
standardized diagnostic investigations and large prospective studies
