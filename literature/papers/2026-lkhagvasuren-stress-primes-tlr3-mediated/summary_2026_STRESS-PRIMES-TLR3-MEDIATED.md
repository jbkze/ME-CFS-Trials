## Summary

Mice that were chronically stressed and then given a molecule that mimics a viral infection developed long-lasting fatigue, pain sensitivity, memory problems and reduced social behaviour — mirroring post-viral fatigue syndrome. A drug that calms overactive immune cells in the brain (minocycline) partly reversed these effects.

## Why this matters

It gives a controlled animal model showing that prior stress plus a viral-like immune trigger can produce lasting ME/CFS-like symptoms through brain inflammation, and that damping that brain-immune response can partly undo it — supporting neuroinflammation as a treatable driver of post-viral fatigue.

## Notes

Confirmed via Europe PMC (PMID 42401313) and Crossref; print issue Sep 2026, online Jul 4, 2026. Animal (mouse) model, not a human study — relevant to the neuroinflammation/microglia mechanism thread; the minocycline result is a treatable-target signal, not a human trial result.
