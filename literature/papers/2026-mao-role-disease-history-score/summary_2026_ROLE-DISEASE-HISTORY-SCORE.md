## Summary

Using health survey data from over ten thousand US adults, researchers found that people who had been depressed in the past were more likely to currently have ME/CFS. Much of this link could be explained by a broader pattern of past illnesses and health problems (a combined 'disease history score'), rather than depression itself directly causing ME/CFS — and the link was stronger in women and older adults.

## Why this matters

Helps untangle a long-running debate over whether depression and ME/CFS overlap because one causes the other, or because both simply reflect a person's overall burden of past illness — relevant to arguments that ME/CFS should be assessed in its full health context rather than reduced to a psychiatric diagnosis.

## Notes

Frontiers in Public Health 14, published Jul 21, 2026. Cross-sectional mediation analysis of 2021-2023 US NHIS survey data; depression history associated with higher current-ME/CFS odds (OR 1.61), ~44-47% mediated by a composite disease-history/multimorbidity score, effect stronger in women and older adults.
