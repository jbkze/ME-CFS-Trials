## Summary

203 people with long-lasting neurological post-COVID symptoms were given temelimab (an antibody that blocks a protein made by ancient "fossil virus" DNA sequences built into our own genome) or a placebo for 24 weeks. Fatigue improved about the same amount in both groups — the drug gave no extra benefit over placebo on fatigue, thinking, mood, or quality of life.

## Why this matters

A negative but rigorous large trial (NCT05497089) that specifically tested the "reactivated fossil virus protein" (HERV-W envelope protein) theory of post-COVID/ME-CFS-type illness — the same theory raised in this database's already-tracked Perera et al. 2026 endogenous-retrovirus review — and did not support it as a treatable driver of fatigue, redirecting attention to other mechanisms (mitochondrial, vascular, autoantibody).

## Notes

203 participants, 14 sites in Switzerland/Spain/Italy (no German site). Primary outcome (24-wk fatigue, PROMIS): temelimab -3.2 vs placebo -3.8, RR 0.9 (95% CI 0.6-1.2), not significant; no significant difference in secondary outcomes (cognition, anxiety, depression, quality of life). Borderline for this tracker: post-COVID neuropsychiatric-symptom population, not a formal ME/CFS/PEM diagnosis. Not Wirth/Scheibenbogen-affiliated. Confirmed via S4ME trial-report thread citing the Brain Behav Immun DOI and CenterWatch/ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05497089) listing; publisher page itself only resolved to a redirect shell during this check.
