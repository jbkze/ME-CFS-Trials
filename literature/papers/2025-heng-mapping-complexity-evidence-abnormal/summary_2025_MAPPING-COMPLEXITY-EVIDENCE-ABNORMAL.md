## Summary

Comparing blood from ME/CFS patients and healthy people, researchers found several problems at once: immune cells were short on the energy molecule ATP (the cell's fuel), several immune-cell types looked less mature than normal, and blood proteins linked to clotting and blood-vessel reactivity were raised — a sign of blood-vessel (endothelial) dysfunction.

## Why this matters

Brings together energy, immune and blood-vessel abnormalities in ME/CFS in one study, and points toward measurable markers that could eventually help diagnose the illness or targets for treatment.
