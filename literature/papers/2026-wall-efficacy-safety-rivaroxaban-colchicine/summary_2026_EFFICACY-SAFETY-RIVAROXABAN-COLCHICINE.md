# Efficacy and safety of rivaroxaban, colchicine, and famotidine-loratadine with specialist supportive clinical care for fatigue in patients with post-COVID-19 condition in the UK: a multisite, open-label, randomised controlled trial

## Summary

In 778 UK long-COVID patients with fatigue, adding one of three repurposed drugs (an anti-inflammatory, a blood thinner, or an antihistamine combo) to standard care was compared with standard care alone for 12 weeks. Two of the three drugs gave a small extra fatigue improvement over standard care alone, but that edge disappeared by 24 weeks, after the drugs were stopped.

## Why this matters

Borderline for this tracker (participants had general long-COVID fatigue, not a formal ME/CFS/PEM diagnosis), but this is one of the largest repurposed-drug trials run in long-COVID fatigue to date, and its main lesson — that symptom-level drug repurposing gave only a small, temporary benefit — is a caution relevant to similar approaches being tried in ME/CFS, reinforcing the case for treatments that target a specific underlying mechanism instead.

## Notes

Published online Jul 8, 2026 (PMID 42419335). Open-label RCT, 778 participants (colchicine n=192, famotidine+loratadine n=193, rivaroxaban n=197, control n=196), 12 German sites not involved (UK NHS only). Primary outcome (12-wk FAS): colchicine -1.49 (p=0.041), famotidine-loratadine -1.48 (p=0.038), rivaroxaban -1.06 (p=0.139, ns); 24-week scores not significantly different between arms. No ME/CFS diagnostic criteria (PEM/Bell score) used for eligibility — general long-COVID fatigue population. Not Wirth/Scheibenbogen-affiliated. Confirmed via PubMed record.
