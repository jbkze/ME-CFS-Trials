# A Multivariable Plasma Extracellular Vesicle Surface Profile Associated with Post-COVID-19 Syndrome

## Related files
- [[literature/papers/2026-erhart-multivariable-plasma-extracellular-vesicle/citation.bib]]

## Summary

Blood and spinal-fluid samples from 61 people with lasting post-COVID symptoms, 80 fully recovered people, and 10 people with fatigue after other infections were tested for extracellular vesicles (EVs) — tiny particles cells release into the blood, a bit like sealed messages — looking at proteins on their surface. No single surface protein reliably told the groups apart on its own, but a computer model combining small differences across many proteins together could distinguish post-COVID patients from recovered people reasonably well, and this combined "EV score" dropped back down a year later in patients who improved.

## Why this matters

Adds a new type of candidate blood test for post-COVID syndrome — based on particles cells shed, rather than antibodies or hormones — from the same Ulm University team already tracked here for finding no unusual brain immune activity in these patients. Together, the two findings point research away from classic autoimmune brain inflammation and toward other biology (e.g. how cells communicate) as a driver of lasting symptoms.
