## Summary

Muscle precursor cells grown in the lab from stem cells taken from ME/CFS patients showed altered activity in genes involved in cell division, DNA repair, immune response and muscle structure, and used certain nutrients for energy differently than cells from healthy people.

_(Full text in `paper.txt`; the summary above is the curated plain-language abstract — deepen from the full text when reading.)_

## Why this matters

Lab evidence that ME/CFS muscle cells are genuinely different at a molecular level, not just 'deconditioned' — and the authors computationally flag 22 existing drugs (including leflunomide, melatonin and midodrine) as candidates that could be tested faster than developing a new drug, though this is an early, not-yet-peer-reviewed preprint using a lab stem-cell model.
