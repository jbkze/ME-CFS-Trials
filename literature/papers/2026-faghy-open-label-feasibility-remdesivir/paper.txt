Abstract
2
Objective: Treatment of acute COVID-19 with anti-viral and immunomodulator 
3
medications demonstrates a reduced risk of long-term outcomes. To date, remdesivir, 
4
an intravenous (IV) antiviral that prevents RNA transcription, has not been evaluated 
5
in people with Long COVID (LC). This study assessed the feasibility of a five‑day IV 
6
remdesivir intervention for individuals with LC.
7
Methods: Seventy-three participants aged ≥18 years with a confirmed LC diagnosis 
8
were recruited across two sites in the United Kingdom to receive a five‑day course of 
9
IV remdesivir. Primary feasibility outcomes included recruitment, treatment 
10
completion, acceptability, and safety. Exploratory patient-reported (e.g. Fatigue 
11
Assessment Scale [FAS]) and clinical outcomes (e.g. 6-minute walk test [6MWT]) were 
12
also collected.
13
Results: Of 106 individuals screened, 73 were enrolled and 71 (97%) completed the 
14
5-day IV remdesivir regimen. Follow-up assessments were completed by 70 
15
participants (96%), with high completion rates observed across clinical assessments, 
16
patient-reported outcomes and symptom tracking. No severe adverse reactions were 
17
reported. Improvements were also observed in several exploratory patient-reported 
18
and clinical outcomes.
19
Conclusion: We demonstrated the feasibility and acceptability of administering IV 
20
remdesivir in people with LC. Analysis of secondary outcomes suggests therapeutic 
21
promise, but rigorous evaluation in large-scale randomised controlled trials is essential 
22
to determine the true efficacy and clinical utility of intravenous remdesivir in this 
23
population.

Lay Summary 
2
Long COVID (LC) can cause ongoing symptoms like fatigue and reduced physical 
3
ability after a COVID-19 infection. Some medicines used during acute COVID-19 
4
infections, such as antivirals, may lower the risk of long-term problems, but it isn’t clear 
5
whether they can help people who already have Long COVID.
6
This study looked at whether it is practical and safe to give remdesivir (an antiviral 
7
drug given through a drip in the arm) to people with Long COVID over five days.
8
A total of 73 adults with Long COVID took part across two sites in the UK. Almost all 
9
participants (97%) were able to complete the full treatment. About one-third 
10
experienced side effects, but none were serious. Researchers also saw signs of 
11
improvement in symptoms like fatigue and in physical performance tests, although 
12
these were not the main focus of the study.
13
Overall, the study shows that giving remdesivir to people with Long COVID is feasible 
14
and generally well tolerated. While early results suggest it might help, larger and more 
15
rigorous studies are needed to find out if it truly works.

Introduction
2
Post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection, also termed post-COVID-19 condition 
3
or Long COVID (LC), is a heterogeneous multisystem disorder characterised by 
4
symptoms lasting at least three months after acute infection, without an alternative 
5
explanation [1]. Global prevalence remains difficult to ascertain due to inconsistent 
6
diagnostic criteria and reduced surveillance, but current estimates suggest that LC 
7
affects between 65 and 400 million people worldwide [2,3].  Distinct subtypes of LC 
8
have been described. Some result from direct viral injury to cardiovascular or 
9
neurological systems, while others reflect sequelae proportional to acute illness 
10
severity, such as post-ICU syndromes. One of the most debilitating presentations is 
11
the myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) phenotype, 
12
characterised by disproportionate fatigue, unrefreshing sleep, cognitive dysfunction 
13
(also known as ‘brain fog’), and post-exertional malaise (PEM, [4] PEM complicates 
14
the assessment and treatment, as even minimal physical, cognitive, orthostatic and 
15
emotional activity can trigger exacerbated symptoms. In research settings, PEM can 
16
be quantified using two-day cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET), where 
17
individuals show reduced function on the second test despite identical exertion [5]. 
18
Although advances have been made in characterising the clinical  spectrum, 
19
epidemiology, and pathophysiology of LC, its underlying mechanisms remain 
20
incompletely defined [6]. Current evidence supports multiple interacting processes, 
21
including persistent viral reservoirs, dysregulated immunity, autoimmunity, latent virus 
22
reactivation, endothelial and microvascular dysfunction, microclots, gut dysbiosis, and 
23
impaired mitochondrial metabolism [7,8]. Neurobiological studies additionally implicate 
24
chronic neuroinflammation, microglial activation, disrupted neurovascular integrity, 
25
and altered synaptic remodelling, contributing to cognitive impairment, sensory 
26
symptoms, and fatigue [9]. 
27
Viral persistence, whether through ongoing low-level replication, residual viral 
28
genomes, or delayed antigen clearance, has been hypothesised as a contributing 
29
factor in the pathology of LC [8]. Persistent SARS‑CoV‑2 antigens and ribonucleic acid 
30
(RNA) have been detected across tissues even after resolution of acute symptoms, 
31
supporting the premise that a subset of individuals with LC are not able to fully clear 
32
viral material [10,11].  Similar persistent viral remnants are well recognised in other 
33
single-stranded RNA infections, including Ebola, Zika, enteroviruses, and measles

2
have also been reported in tissues and post-mortem samples, reinforcing the 
3
biological plausibility of sustained viral reservoirs [13–18]. 
4
The role of antiviral therapeutics in LC is driven by accumulating evidence that 
5
persistent 
viral 
reservoirs 
or 
viral 
products 
may 
contribute 
to 
ongoing 
6
symptomatology.[8] Antiviral therapy, whether specific for SARS-CoV-2 with 
7
nirmatrelvir (administered with ritonavir to improve bioavailability, marketed as 
8
Paxlovid) or repurposed from other disease areas (e.g., molnupiravir, tenofovir 
9
disoproxil/emtricitabine (TDF/FTC) or maraviroc), when administered in acute SARS-
10
CoV-2 have reduced the incidence of LC [19–22]. To date, however, oral therapies 
11
have failed to demonstrate clinically meaningful benefits in LC, even after extended 
12
dosing strategies [19,23].
13
Remdesivir is an IV nucleotide analogue prodrug that acts by inhibiting the viral 
14
RNA‑dependent RNA polymerase [24]. This posology allows for higher intracellular 
15
concentrations over a longer duration than can be achieved with oral formulations. It 
16
does, however, require consecutive days of treatment, which exerts additional 
17
limitations on a population living with chronic and debilitating symptoms, including 
18
post-exertional malaise. We therefore sought to determine the recruitment, retention 
19
and tolerability of a trial using this treatment regimen. Trial feasibility and the 
20
determination of progression to a definitive trial were the primary outcomes. To 
21
appropriately power the definitive clinical trial, we collected clinically relevant patient-
22
reported outcome measures and clinical assessments for descriptive analysis.

Methods 
24
Study Design
25
ERASE-LC was a two-centre (Derby and Exeter, UK), open-label, single-arm 
26
feasibility trial evaluating the deliverability, acceptability, and tolerability of a five-day 
27
IV remdesivir regimen in adults with LC. The full protocol, schedule of assessments, 
28
and prespecified progression (‘stop–go’) criteria have been published previously [25]. 
29
The trial was registered on relevant trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05911906; 
30
ISRCTN 72940450). The study was conducted in accordance with Good Clinical 
31
Practice and reported in line with Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials 
32
(CONSORT) guidance for pilot and feasibility trials. Ethics approval was obtained from

Participants
6
Eligible participants were adults (≥18 years) with clinician-confirmed LC (post-COVID-
7
19 condition) and previous confirmed or suspected SARS-CoV-2 infection, with 
8
persistent symptoms relative to pre-COVID baseline as captured by patient-reported 
9
outcomes. Additional inclusion criteria were the ability to provide informed consent, 
10
complete study questionnaires and planned clinical assessments, attend scheduled 
11
visits, and live within commutable distance of the study site (at the discretion of the 
12
local principal investigator). Full criteria are provided in the protocol [25]. Key exclusion 
13
criteria were prior remdesivir, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, molnupiravir, or other COVID-19 
14
antiviral use within the preceding 6 months; known immunocompromise; current 
15
participation in a structured physical rehabilitation programme aimed at LC; inability to 
16
complete procedures due to severe risk of PEM as judged clinically by the Modified 
17
DePaul Symptom Questionnaire (DSQ-PEM, 26], pregnancy, breastfeeding, or 
18
attempting to conceive; unable to understand verbal English/have a hearing 
19
impairment that prevents adequate communication, participation in another clinical 
20
drug trial within the last 6 months, taking concomitant medications with known clinically 
21
significant interactions with remdesivir; history of serious infusion reactions; and 
22
clinically significant hepatic or renal impairment. 
23
Patient and Public Involvement and Engagement (PPIE) 
24
People with lived experience of LC contributed to all aspects of the trial, and this was 
25
achieved through an established PPIE group. Involvement was critical at all stages of 
26
the study, including review of participant-facing materials and developing study and 
27
site-specific safety mitigations (including reducing or eliminating the risk of PEM, and 
28
exposure to airborne and fomite pathogens) whilst undertaking all aspects of the trial. 
29
PPIE representatives were core members of the trial leadership team and had 
30
representation in trial oversight structures (e.g., Trial Management Group and Trial 
31
Steering Committee), ensuring that the study maintained a patient‑centred focus.

Participants attended their local site on five consecutive days for IV remdesivir. Visits 
2
included assessment of adverse events since prior contact, infusion administration, 
3
and post-infusion observation. Participants able to conceive underwent pregnancy 
4
testing at each dosing visit. Remdesivir was administered per the Summary of Product 
5
Characteristics: a 200 mg loading dose on day 1 (IV infusion over 60 minutes), 
6
followed by 100 mg on days 2–5 (IV infusion over 30 minutes), each in 250 mL 0·9% 
7
sodium chloride. Participants were observed for at least 30 minutes after each 
8
infusion. Predefined stopping criteria included clinically significant infusion reactions 
9
or other safety concerns judged by the treating clinician; participants discontinuing 
10
treatment were withdrawn from further dosing but were invited to complete follow-up 
11
assessments where feasible.
12
Procedures and assessments
13
Assessments were completed at baseline and post-intervention follow-up 
14
(approximately 28 days after completion of dosing). Patient-reported outcome 
15
measures (PROMs) were completed electronically (REDCap) with paper alternatives 
16
available as a reasonable adjustment. PROMs included fatigue (Fatigue Assessment 
17
Scale [FAS] and Modified Fatigue Impact Scale [MFIS]), health-related quality of life 
18
(EQ-5D-5L), symptom burden (Symptom Burden Questionnaire for LC [SBQ™-LC]), 
19
functional status (Post-COVID Functional Status scale), dyspnoea (Medical Research 
20
Council dyspnoea scale), cognitive symptoms (Perceived Deficit Questionnaire [PDQ-
21
5]), generalized anxiety disorder questionnaire (GAD-7) and PEM (DSQ-PEM). 
22
Clinical assessments included the six-minute walk test (6MWT) with Borg perceived 
23
exertion and oxygen saturation, venous blood sampling for inflammatory and 
24
metabolic biomarkers, and a two-day CPET protocol (Exeter site only) to quantify 
25
exercise capacity and exertional responses (full details in protocol [25]. Participants 
26
also undertook daily symptom tracking using a smartphone application (Visible Health, 
27
UK), where possible and those without smartphone access used paper diaries. 
28
Additional symptom-tracking and wearable-monitoring and physiologic data were 
29
collected as part of the wider feasibility programme; detailed analyses of these 
30
datasets are beyond the scope of the present manuscript and will be reported 
31
separately.

Statistical Analysis
10
Participant-reported and clinical outcomes were summarised according to the 
11
intention-to-treat principle (i.e. including all participants regardless of adherence to the 
12
study drug). This feasibility trial was not powered to support or justify any conclusions 
13
regarding treatment efficacy/effectiveness deduced from any hypothesis testing. The 
14
analysis did not include formal inferential statistical comparisons or hypothesis testing. 
15
All analyses were undertaken once the final participant had completed their final 
16
assessment, and the database was locked. Baseline characteristics collected during 
17
screening after consent were summarised descriptively by recruitment site to provide 
18
an overview of the trial population. Continuous data were summarised by mean and 
19
standard deviation (SD), unless data were at least moderately skewed, in which case, 
20
the median and interquartile range were used. Categorical variables were summarised 
21
by frequencies and percentages of non-missing responses. Pre-post intervention 
22
differences were summarised and presented with two-sided 75%, 85% and 95% 
23
confidence intervals (CIs). Analyses were adjusted for site.

8

9

10

11

12
intolerance, sleep disturbances, and neurocognitive impairment. To date, no antiviral 
13
has yet shown consistent, robust efficacy across diverse patient populations; indeed, 
14
several large randomised controlled trials of the MPro inhibitor nirmatrelvir, packaged 
15
with ritonavir as Paxlovid, have failed to show benefit [38]. Paxlovid, however, is 
16
dependent on actively replicating SARS-CoV-2. Although particles of viral RNA and 
17
SARS-CoV-2 antigen have been identified in people with LC, active viral replication 
18
has not been identified. Remdesivir is a direct genomic RNA synthesis inhibitor that 
19
impairs SARS-CoV-2 replication [39].  Administered IV, in a pro-drug form, it is then 
20
converted intracellularly into the active triphosphate, where it acts on viral RNA 
21
transcription. Importantly, however, it is not dependent on whole viral replication to be 
22
effective, but rather it inhibits RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, which plays a key 
23
role in viral RNA transcription. It is feasible that this may reduce antigen formation from 
24
retained viral RNA fragments even in the absence of viral replication. This gives it a 
25
biologically plausible mechanism for reducing inflammatory burden, where other 
26
antivirals, dependent on preventing whole viral replication, have failed.

32

16

20

21
