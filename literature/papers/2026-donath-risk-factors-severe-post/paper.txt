SUMMARY 
Background 
Post-COVID condition (PCC) in children and young people (PCCcyp) remains a significant 
health burden. Early identification of patients at risk for severe disease, including myalgic 
encephalomyelitis/ chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), is crucial to provide timely and 
adequate care. 
Methods 
This monocentric, observational registry study was performed at a tertiary pediatric hospital in 
Germany. Children and young people aged 7–25 years with post-COVID condition (PCC), 
according to the WHO, were included at the time of diagnosis. Standardized clinical 
assessment tools and patient-reported outcome measures were applied, including the novel 
Munich Long COVID Symptom Questionnaire (MLCSQ). Severe PCC was defined by 
clustered chronic symptom burden, fatigue severity scale (FSS), Total Composite Autonomic 
Symptom Score-31 (COMPASS-31), SF-36 composite scores, Bell Score, and confirmed 
ME/CFS diagnosis.  
Findings 
Of 120 participants, severe PCCcyp was associated with a higher number of acute symptoms 
(adjusted OR 1·22), acute orthostatic intolerance (adjusted OR 9·87), acute trouble 
concentrating (adjusted OR 11·8), and female sex (OR 3·31). Categorising acute symptoms 
at a threshold of ≥12 yielded the best model performance (AUC 0·857; sensitivity 65·6%; 
specificity 90·2%). ME/CFS was diagnosed in 24% participants, all within the severe PCC 
cluster, and was characterised by greater acute symptom complexity, more fatigue, more 
autonomic symptoms, and poorer physical function. 
Interpretation 
The number of acute symptoms and/or individual symptoms during the acute phase of the 
SARS-CoV-2 infection serve as early and specific predictors for severe PCCcyp. In particular, 
patients with ≥12 acute symptoms should be closely monitored to enable early diagnosis of 
severe PCC and potentially ME/CFS. A distinct cluster of severely affected patients – 
frequently with ME/CFS diagnosis – was identified.

Research in context 
Evidence before this study 
Post-COVID condition (PCC) in children and young people (CYP) has been increasingly 
recognized, but robust evidence on predictors of severe disease has remained scarce. 
Previous reports in adult patients documented a wide spectrum of persistent symptoms, 
frequently including fatigue, neurocognitive complaints, and autonomic dysfunction, yet no 
study systematically investigated acute-phase predictors for severe PCCcyp.  
 
Added value of this study 
In this monocentric observational registry study on 120 CYP aged 7–25 years with PCC, we 
identified acute-phase risk factors for severe pediatric PCC. We show that a high number of 
acute symptoms (≥12 symptoms), female sex, acute orthostatic intolerance, and/or acute 
trouble concentrating strongly predicted a severe PCCcyp cluster. Importantly, 24% of patients 
fulfilled ME/CFS diagnostic criteria, and all clustered within the severe PCC group, 
underscoring the clinical overlap between severe pediatric PCC and ME/CFS. 
 
Implications of all the available evidence 
These findings provide a framework for early risk stratification in pediatric PCC. Recognising 
acute predictors is crucial to provide timely and adequate care, particularly to those with 12 or 
more acute symptoms and/or specific symptoms during the acute SARS-CoV-2 infection. The 
identification of a clinically relevant ME/CFS subgroup underscores the need to integrate 
ME/CFS expertise into pediatric PCC care pathways. Together, this evidence can inform 
clinical management, healthcare planning, and the design of interventional trials targeting the 
most vulnerable CYP with PCC.

INTRODUCTION 
1 
Post-acute sequelae of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) (PASC), often referred to as 
2 
Long COVID, represent major clinical and economic challenges worldwide.1-4 They include 
3 
ongoing, relapsing, or new symptoms at four weeks or later after infection with severe acute 
4 
respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2).5 A chronic form of PASC has been defined 
5 
by the WHO as Post-COVID Condition (PCC). According to the definition for children and 
6 
adolescents, PCC occurs in individuals with a history of confirmed or probable SARS-CoV-2 
7 
infection, when experiencing symptoms lasting at least 2 months which initially occurred within 
8 
3 months of acute COVID-19.  
9 
PCC can cause symptoms across all organ systems and often significantly impacts 
10 
functioning, participation, and health-related quality of life (HRQoL).6 Frequent symptoms 
11 
include fatigue, exertional intolerance, headache, respiratory symptoms, cognitive dysfunction, 
12 
including brain fog, sleep problems, loss of smell and taste, anxiety, and autonomic 
13 
dysfunction.7,8 The latter can manifest as orthostatic intolerance (OI) with or without postural 
14 
orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (PoTS).9 Some patients develop post-exertional malaise 
15 
(PEM), a compromising condition with symptom worsening after daily activities that were 
16 
tolerated before. Long-lasting PEM is a cardinal symptom of myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic 
17 
fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), a chronic multisystemic disease defined by clinical criteria and 
18 
mostly triggered by viral infections.5,9,10 Most recent studies estimate ME/CFS prevalences 
19 
ranging from 4·5 to 11·6% in adult PCC patients, depending on the study design.11 Diagnosing 
20 
PCC, PoTS, and ME/CFS requires a thorough diagnostic work-up to define clinical phenotypes 
21 
and rule out other medical conditions with symptom overlap.  
22 
Data on PCC in CYP (PCCcyp) is still scarce, and age-specific factors are insufficiently 
23 
understood.5 Depending on the study design, the reported PASC prevalence in minors ranged 
24 
from 0·8% to 60%,12 with lower rates observed in studies with SARS-CoV-2-negative control 
25 
groups.6,12-15 While most CYP recover within a few months, a subset develops long-term 
26 
disease.3,7,15,16  
27 
Established risk factors for PCC in adults include infection with pre-Omicron variants,3 older 
28 
age, female sex, pre-existing chronic conditions, lack of COVID-19 vaccination,17 more severe 
29 
acute illness, and greater symptom complexity.18 Preliminary evidence suggests similar risk 
30 
patterns in CYP,19-22 but risk factors of PCCcyp are more difficult to assess due to limited data 
31 
and heterogenous age groups.  
32 
The IMMUC study on EBV-associated infectious mononucleosis (EBV-IM) and its long-term 
33 
sequelae in CYP indicated that susceptibility to infection, a higher number of acute symptoms, 
34 
and early gastrointestinal (GI) involvement predicted prolonged recovery and chronic fatigue.23 
35 
Especially, gastrointestinal symptoms even prior to EBV primary infection has been identified 
36 
as a risk factor for severe ME/CFS. 
37 
Here, we report on clinical phenotypes during the acute and chronic phases after SARS-COV-
38 
2 infection of 120 CYP, including patients with post-COVID ME/CFS. We provide the first 
39 
evidence for clinical risk factors of severe PCCcyp, which might facilitate timely identification 
40 
and adequate care.

METHODS 
42 
Study Cohort and Case Definition 
43 
This monocenter observational registry study comprises medical data from 120 CYP aged 7 
44 
to 25 years and diagnosed with PCC according to WHO definitions24 between January 2021 
45 
and May 2024 at the Munich Chronic Fatigue Center for Young People (MCFC).  
46 
Confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection was defined by positive PCR or rapid antigen test combined 
47 
with typical COVID-19 symptoms, while probable SARS-CoV-2 infection was defined by 
48 
COVID-19 symptoms after contact with a SARS-CoV-2 PCR-positive person and positive 
49 
SARS-CoV-2 serology not explained by vaccination. 
50 
Confirmed MECFS was defined by fulfilling the criteria of the Institute of Medicine (IOM) and/or 
51 
the Canadian Consensus Criteria (CCC) as assessed by the Munich Berlin Symptom 
52 
Questionnaire (MBSQ)9 in a structured medical interview after symptom-oriented differential 
53 
diagnosis. In case of pediatric “pediatric case definition” of L.A. Jason and colleagues (PCD-
54 
J) and/or the clinical diagnostic worksheet designed by P.C. Rowe and colleagues (CDW-R)10 
55 
were used. 
56 
 
57 
Assessed Data 
58 
Patients were asked to fill in questionnaires pre-admission, retrospectively addressing their 
59 
and their family’s general medical history, their symptoms during the acute phase of the SARS-
60 
CoV-2 infection as well as their current symptoms, medical treatment, grade of participation in 
61 
education/work, and HRQoL. To assess PCC symptoms, diagnostic ME/CFS criteria, severity 
62 
of fatigue, presence, severity, and duration of PEM, autonomic symptoms, the daily activity 
63 
level, and HRQoL we used the novel Munich Long COVID Symptom Questionnaire (MLCSQ), 
64 
MBSQ9, the fatigue severity scale (FSS),25 the DePaul Symptom Questionnaire for Post-
65 
exertional Malaise (DSQ-PEM),26 the Composite Autonomic Symptom Score 31 (COMPASS 
66 
31),27 the Bell CFIDS disability scale (Bell Score), and the Short Form-36 Health Survey (SF-
67 
36) with Physical (PCS) and Mental Component Summary (MCS),28 respectively. 
68 
The MLCSQ was developed for structured age-independent assessment of PCC symptoms in 
69 
13 domains, encompassing 83 items (Supplementary Material M1, Appendix pp.7-9). Items 
70 
were adapted from the WHO Global COVID-19 Clinical Platform Case Report Form (CRF) for 
71 
PCC (2021), the Long COVID-19 Survey by the German Society for Pediatric Infectious 
72 
Diseases (DGPI, 2021), and the COMPASS 31 (Sletten et al., 2012).27 Frequency and severity 
73 
were quantitatively assessed using a Likert scale as “not anymore/intermittent/persistent” or 
74 
“mild/moderate/severe”, respectively. Only symptoms rated as “intermittent” or “persistent” in 
75 
frequency were considered as PCC symptoms in this study.  
76

79

80

81

82 
statistically significant.

Identification and Risk Factor Analysis for Severe PCC Cluster: A data-driven clustering 
93 
approach was applied to identify patient subgroups based on PCC severity. The following 
94 
outcome variables were used for the cluster definition: Bell Score, FSS, COMPASS 31 total 
95 
score, SF36-PCS, SF36-MCS, number of PCC symptoms, ME/CFS diagnosis. A k-means 
96 
clustering algorithm (k=2) was applied to the standardized data set. Two clusters 
97 
encompassed patients with “moderate” and “severe” manifestations of these outcome 
98 
variables, referred to as “moderate PCC” or “severe PCC”, respectively, for easy 
99 
interpretability. The cluster membership was used as the primary outcome for subsequent 
100 
analyses. 
101

102 
the acute COVID-19 phase and subsequent cluster membership. Candidate predictor 
103 
variables were the same as for the risk factor analysis using distinct clinical outcomes (see 
104 
above).  
105

106 
optimal penalty parameter (λ) with the least deviance. To account for class imbalance between 
107 
the “mild PCC” and “severe PCC” clusters, inverse class weights were applied.

RESULTS 
136 
Patient Characteristics 
137 
120 CYP with PCC included 16/120 (13%) children (7-11 years), 71/12 (59%) adolescents (12-
138 
17 years), and 33/12 (28%) young adults (18-25 years). The percentage of females 
139 
significantly increased with age (13% children, 62% adolescents, and 76% adults) (Table 1).  
140 
 
141 
Characteristics of the Acute Phase of the SARS-CoV-2 Infection 
142 
Documented or probable SARS-CoV-2 infections occurred between February 2020 and 
143 
January 2023, spanning pandemic waves in Germany with dominant Alpha (43/114 (38%)), 
144 
Delta (25/114 (22%)), and Omicron variants (46/114 (40%)).  
145 
112/120 (93%) and 8/120 (7%) patients reported their first or second SARS-CoV-2 infection 
146 
as PCC trigger, respectively. The triggering SARS-CoV-2 infection was indicated by PCR in 
147 
106/120 (88%), rapid antigen testing in 8/120 (7%), or serology together with contact in 6/120 
148 
(5%) CYP. 82/116 (71%) were not COVID-19 vaccinated prior to the index infection. A 
149 
triggering breakthrough infection was reported in 34/116 (29%) patients (Table 1). 
150 
All patients remembered a median number of 16 (IQR: 11-21) acute COVID-19 symptoms 
151 
during the index infection, with no significant difference between the three age groups (P = 
152 
0·579). The five most frequently reported COVID-19 symptoms were fatigue, headaches, sore 
153 
throat, persistent dry cough, and trouble concentrating (Table 1). Loss of appetite (P = 0·017) 
154 
and diarrhea (P < 0·001) were significantly more frequent in children. Heart racing (P = 0·030), 
155 
loss of smell (P < 0·001) of taste (P = 0·003) were significantly more frequent in adolescents 
156 
and young adults. Only 9/117 (8%) patients were hospitalized for their index infection (Table 
157 
1). 
158 
 
159 
Characteristics of Post-COVID-Condition 
160 
Time from acute symptom onset to diagnosis was 8·2 month in median (IQR: 5·4 - 12·4) and 
161 
significantly greater for young adults compare to children and adolescents (P = 0·007). 
162 
Patients presented with various PCC-associated medical diagnoses (Suppl. Table S1, 
163 
Appendix p.2). The three most frequent were fatigue (ICD-10-GM R53) (81/120, 68%), brain 
164 
fog (ICD-10-GM F06.7) (64/120, 53%), and headaches (ICD-10-GM R51) (49/120, 41%). Brain 
165 
fog (ICD-10-GM F06.7) was significantly more frequent in young adults compared to children 
166 
and adolescents (P = 0·034).  
167 
 
168 
The median number of PCC symptoms at the time of diagnosis was 29 (IQR: 21 – 37) and the 
169 
most frequent symptoms were fatigue, exercise intolerance, reduced endurance, troubles in 
170 
concentrating, and headaches, as indicated by the MLCSQ (Table 2). The median FSS score 
171 
was 6·4 (IQR 5·8 to 6·6), reflecting pronounced fatigue (Table 3). The DSQ-PEM indicated 
172 
PEM in 112/119 (94%) patients, with a duration of 14-23 or ≥24 hours reported by 19/114 
173 
(17%) and 23/114 (20%) patients, respectively (Table 3). The COMPASS 31 revealed a 
174 
median score for OI of 20·0 (12·0 - 28·0) and a median total score of 27·8 (17·8 - 38·8), 
175 
reflecting severe autonomic distress (Table 3).  
176 
The median Bell Score was 40% (IQR 30 to 60), indicating significant impairment of daily life 
177 
(Table 3). 40/119 (34%) and 29/119 (24%) CYP stated to have less than 50% or no 
178 
participation in school or work, respectively (Table 3). SF-36 median scores were markedly 
179 
reduced in all eight dimensions compared to a German normative sample (aged 14–20 years, 
180 
N = 228),  with a median PCS of 31·0 (27·2 - 36·1) versus 57·1 (54·6 – 58·8) (P < 0·025) and 
181 
a median MCS of 44·2 (35·1 - 51·0) versus 53·5 (50·4 – 55·9) (P < 0·114) (Table 3). PoTS 
182 
(ICD-10-GM G90·80) was diagnosed in 25/120 (21%) patients (Table 3).

was further characterized by a lower Bell Score, more severe fatigue (FSS), more autonomic

symptoms (COMPASS 31), more PCC symptoms, a lower SF36-PCS and a lower SF36-MCS 
226 
compared to the second cluster (Suppl. Figure S1B-S1H). Female sex, but not age, had a 
227 
significantly greater likelihood of being attributed to the severe PCC cluster (OR = 3·31, P = 
228 
0·031). Adjusted for sex and age, the number of acute symptoms (adj. OR = 1·22, P < 0·001), 
229 
acute symptoms of OI (adj. OR = 9·87, P = 0·002), and acute troubles concentrating (adj. OR 
230 
= 11·8, P = 0·005) were significantly associated with the severe PCC cluster. (Figure 1) 
231 
A trained and cross-validated LASSO retained the following predictors a final logistic 
232 
regression model: female sex (coefficient: 1·08), age (coefficient: -0·07), hospitalization during 
233 
acute COVID-19 (coefficient: 0·10), number of acute COVID-19 symptoms (coefficient: 0·10), 
234 
acute OI symptoms (coefficient: 1·09), acute fatigue (coefficient: -0·95), and acute troubles 
235 
concentrating (coefficient: 0·80). A ROC analysis of this model resulted in an AUC of 85·7%. 
236 
The optimal threshold for predicting severe PCC was if the predicted probability p ≥63·8% 
237 
(Youden index; Suppl. Figure S1I). The model reliably detected CYP with “severe PCC” 
238 
(sensitivity: 65·6%) or “moderate PCC” (specificity: 90·2%), yielding an overall accuracy of 
239 
80·0% (balanced accuracy: 77·9%) (Suppl. Figure S1J, S1K). A binary categorization of the 
240 
acute number of symptoms in the penalized logistic regression model revealed that ≥12 acute 
241 
COVID-19 symptoms had the best model fit, indicated by minimal cross-validated model error 
242 
(Suppl. Figure S1L).

DISCUSSION 
244 
This study identified key risk factors for severe PCC in CYP after confirmed or probable 
245 
COVID-19, highlighted a distinct subgroup of highly affected individuals, and introduced the 
246 
novel MLCSQ as a robust instrument for standardized assessment of PCC symptoms. The 
247 
most important finding was that a high number of acute symptoms and the presence of 
248 
autonomic symptoms during the acute phase of initial SARS-CoV-2 infection were early and 
249 
specific predictors of severe PCC and thereby may allow for tailored and timely symptom-
250 
oriented care. 
251 
A higher number of acute initial symptoms was significantly associated with more severe long-
252 
term outcomes, including more severe fatigue (FSS) and autonomic dysfunction (COMPASS-
253 
31), reduced physical and mental HRQoL (SF-36), and a higher number of chronic symptoms 
254 
(MLCSQ). These results expand on earlier work, which indicated that acute EBV-IM symptom 
255 
complexity and autonomic EBV-IM symptoms predicted prolonged recovery and/or chronic 
256 
fatigue.23 The latter finding was consistent with results from Jason et al., who identified greater 
257 
symptom burden and immune activation as predictors of EBV-IM-driven ME/CFS. 
258 
In adults, the presence of five acute COVID-19 symptoms has been associated with increased 
259 
PCC risk.18 Moreover, symptomatic versus asymptomatic SARS-CoV-2 infection has been 
260 
linked to long-term sequelae in adults and children. While earlier pediatric studies identified 
261 
hospitalization for COVID-193,19,20 and acute complications such as pneumonia, reduced 
262 
oxygen saturation, and hypotension as risk factors for PCC, our data are the first to 
263 
demonstrate that a threshold of ≥12 COVID-19 symptoms allows for identifying CYP at risk of 
264 
severe PCC. 
265 
Among acute COVID-19 symptoms, symptoms of OI (dizziness, syncope, blackouts, and 
266 
circulatory dysregulation) emerged as another strong predictor of severe PCC. These 
267 
symptoms were significantly associated with increased fatigue, greater autonomic distress, 
268 
poorer mental health, and a higher number of symptoms in the context of PCC. This supports 
269 
the hypothesis that autonomic dysregulation plays a key role in the development of post-viral 
270 
sequelae.14 
271 
Unsupervised clustering delineated a clinically coherent “severe” phenotype that concentrated 
272 
all ME/CFS cases and showed markedly worse fatigue, autonomic symptom burden, functional 
273 
status, and HRQoL than the comparison cluster. This supports the robustness of our cluster 
274 
endpoint.  
275 
The risk-factor analyses found that female sex was associated with higher odds of severe 
276 
PCC, which is consistent with prior pediatric reports.8 In contrast to several pediatric cohort 
277 
studies reporting, we could not confirm age as a relevant risk factor for PCC. 3 E.g., Rao et al. 
278 
found that children younger than five years were more strongly associated with PASC.19 This 
279 
controversy might indicate that more nuanced age-specific modelling is needed. Acute 
280 
orthostatic-intolerance symptoms and acute troubles concentrating exhibited the strongest 
281 
adjusted associations with severe PCC, highlighting early autonomic and neurocognitive 
282 
involvement as salient signals. A higher acute symptom burden also tracked with risk, with the 
283 
penalized model indicating ≥12 acute symptoms as a pragmatic cut-point. It is not surprising 
284 
that acute fatigue was not identified as a discriminatory risk factor since it was prevalent in 
285 
90% of all patients. 
286 
LASSO-penalized logistic regression selected seven independent predictors for severe PCC, 
287 
including female sex, younger age, hospitalization, number of acute symptoms, acute OI 
288 
symptoms, acute troubles concentrating, and absence of acute fatigue. In accordance, female 
289 
sex has been considered a risk factor in pediatric patients.8 Interestingly, acute fatigue and sex 
290 
now appeared as risk factors. However, they may merely reflect an additional effect in the

Strengths and Limitations 
300 
A key strength of our work is its clinical immediacy: we identify a pragmatic threshold, i.e., ≥12 
301 
acute symptoms, that enables early risk stratification for severe PCC in CYP. The study 
302 
leverages a relatively large, well-characterized pediatric cohort and a comprehensive, 
303 
multidimensional assessment using validated clinical instruments and PROMs, enhancing 
304 
reproducibility and comparability. Our risk model is robust and data-driven, combining 
305 
unsupervised clustering with LASSO and cross-validation to prioritize predictors with joint 
306 
explanatory value. Finally, the retained predictors (sex, age, hospitalization, acute symptom 
307 
load, acute OI symptoms, acute concentration problems, and acute fatigue) are easy to 
308 
assess, supporting direct clinical translation. 
309 
 
310 
Unfortunately, this analysis did not include a control group that fully recovered after SARS-
311 
CoV-2 infection. Therefore, risk factors were conditional on PCC development, and general 
312 
extrapolation to CYP with SARS-CoV-2 infections remains limited. Moreover, COVID-19 
313 
symptoms might be influenced by recall bias, and the proposed clinical cut-off of ≥12 acute 
314 
symptoms requires further validation, including age- and sex-specific analyses in larger, 
315 
independent cohorts. Additional limitations include missing data in some outcome variables, 
316 
which reduced the effective sample size for clustering and regression analyses and may have 
317 
affected the stability of model estimates. Lastly, findings for our German monocenter cohort 
318 
may not be generalizable to other populations or healthcare systems. 
319 
 
320 
This study highlights the importance of acute-phase symptom profiles, particularly the number 
321 
of symptoms and autonomic dysfunction, for identifying CYP at risk for severe PCC. The novel 
322 
MLCSQ enables a standardized, quantitative assessment of PCC symptom load. Our findings 
323 
emphasize the need for systematic early symptom documentation to guide timely risk 
324 
stratification, individualized follow-up, and targeted intervention in pediatric populations 
325 
affected by long-term sequelae of COVID-19.
