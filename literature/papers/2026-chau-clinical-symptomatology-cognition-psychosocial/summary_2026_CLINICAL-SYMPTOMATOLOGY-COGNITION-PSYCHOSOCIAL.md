# Clinical Symptomatology, Cognition and Psychosocial Wellbeing in Adolescents With Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome: A Cross-Sectional Observational Study

## Related files

## Summary

Researchers in Melbourne compared 25 teenagers with mild-to-moderate ME/CFS to 25 healthy teens. The ME/CFS group's overall intelligence was normal, but they were measurably slower at processing information, and reported much worse sleep, quality of life, anxiety and depression; parents also noticed attention and memory problems.

## Why this matters

Shows the "brain fog" many young ME/CFS patients describe is a real, measurable slowdown in mental processing speed rather than lower intelligence — supporting flexible school accommodations rather than assuming affected teens just need to try harder.
