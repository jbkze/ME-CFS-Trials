## Summary

Using brain-wave recordings (ERPs) during sound tests in 31 people with ME/CFS and 30 healthy people, basic sound processing in the brain was normal, but a later brain signal linked to attention and mental effort was weaker in the ME/CFS patients.

_(Full text in `paper.txt`; the summary above is the curated plain-language abstract — deepen from the full text when reading.)_

## Why this matters

Suggests sensory overload in ME/CFS comes from how the brain manages attention and higher-level processing rather than from broken basic senses — pointing future treatments toward this higher-level brain function rather than the sense organs themselves.
