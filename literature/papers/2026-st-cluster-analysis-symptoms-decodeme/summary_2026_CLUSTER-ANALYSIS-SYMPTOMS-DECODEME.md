## Summary

Using symptom surveys from over 19,000 people with ME/CFS in the UK's large DecodeME study, researchers found patients split into two broad groups: one (just over half) with a heavier symptom load across nearly every body system, and one with milder symptoms. People who got sick after an infection (rather than a gradual, unclear onset) were more likely to be in the heavier-symptom group, and so were women.

## Why this matters

This helps confirm that ME/CFS is not one single illness but at least two distinguishable patterns tied to how someone got sick — information that could help future drug trials pick more consistent patient groups. No specific gene explained the split, so the cause of the two patterns is still unclear.
