# Agreement and reliability between the two-day 6-minute incremental step test and two-day cardiopulmonary exercise test in post COVID-19 condition for assessing post-exertional malaise: The REVEAL-study

## Related files
- [[literature/papers/2026-bomans-agreement-reliability-between-two/citation.bib]]

## Summary

Researchers tested whether a simpler, cheaper 6-minute stepping test, done on two separate days, could replace the demanding two-day cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET — a treadmill/bike test measuring oxygen use) for detecting post-exertional malaise (PEM, the crash after activity) in 25 people with post-COVID condition. The simpler step test only partly agreed with the CPET results, especially for oxygen-use and muscle-symptom measures, so it isn't yet reliable enough to fully substitute for the more demanding test.

## Why this matters

Finding an easier, less exhausting way to objectively measure PEM would make it much simpler to run future ME/CFS and post-COVID drug trials — this study is a needed reality-check showing the simpler option still needs refinement before it can be trusted.
