## Summary

Combining genetic data from two large, separate ME/CFS studies (the UK's DecodeME and the US Million Veteran Program — almost 20,000 patients and 700,000 people without the illness combined), the strongest repeated signals pointed to brain genes, especially ones at 'glutamatergic synapses' (junctions between nerve cells that use the chemical messenger glutamate) — not genes active in blood.

## Why this matters

Independently combining two of the largest ME/CFS genetic datasets and finding the same brain-nerve-signal genes strengthens the case that ME/CFS involves a real, brain-based biological process, and flags specific pathways future drugs could target.
