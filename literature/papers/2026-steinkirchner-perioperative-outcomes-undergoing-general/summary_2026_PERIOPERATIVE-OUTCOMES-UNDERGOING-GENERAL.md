## Summary

Looking back at hospital records for 15 people with ME/CFS who had surgery under general anaesthesia, matched against 15 similar patients without ME/CFS, the study found blood pressure and heart rate during surgery were similarly well controlled in both groups — but people with ME/CFS had noticeably more pain afterward and needed stronger pain medication more often.

## Why this matters

This is practical safety information for patients and doctors: general anaesthesia itself appears manageable in ME/CFS, but post-surgery pain management may need extra attention — useful for anyone with ME/CFS facing an operation, even though it doesn't point to a new drug or trial.

## Notes

Preprint, not yet peer-reviewed, posted Apr 7, 2026. German group (University Hospital Regensburg). Small sample (n=15 pairs); post-exertional malaise not tracked, noted by the authors as a limitation. Not Wirth/Scheibenbogen-affiliated. Confirmed via Europe PMC record.
