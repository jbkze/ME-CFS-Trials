## Summary

Using a cap of electrodes that measures brain electrical activity (EEG), researchers watched the brains of 20 long-COVID patients and 20 healthy people at rest and while doing a hand-grip task. The long-COVID patients showed a smaller brain-activity 'rebound' right after moving and a slower resting brain rhythm over the area that controls movement — patterns linked to fatigue and brain fog (trouble thinking clearly).

## Why this matters

Offers two concrete, measurable brain-wave signals as candidate objective tests for brain fog — a symptom that is otherwise hard to measure or prove to others — which could help diagnose it and track whether future treatments actually help.
