## Summary

In this large US trial, 959 adults with long COVID (symptoms lasting 12+ weeks after COVID-19) were randomly given the antiviral drug Paxlovid (nirmatrelvir-ritonavir) or a dummy pill for 15 or 25 days, to test whether hidden leftover virus is driving their symptoms. Whether people mainly had brain fog, dizziness/fast heart rate, or exercise intolerance (a crash after activity), the drug did not work better than placebo on any measure.

## Why this matters

This is the largest, most rigorous test yet of the idea that long COVID is caused by virus lingering in the body, and it found no benefit — a disappointing but important result that pushes research toward other explanations and treatments, including for the exercise-intolerance/PEM-type long COVID that overlaps with ME/CFS.

## Notes

NCT05595369, NIH RECOVER Consortium. n=959 mITT across 3 pre-specified symptom phenotypes (cognitive n=332, autonomic n=334, exercise/PEM n=332), 69 US sites, enrolled Jul 2023-Sep 2024. Primary endpoint = day-90 change on phenotype-specific PROs (incl. a modified DePaul PEM short form for the exercise arm). No statistically significant benefit on any primary or secondary endpoint for either 15-day or 25-day regimen vs placebo; no safety signal (52 SAEs in 42/963, no deaths). Not Wirth/Scheibenbogen-affiliated but directly relevant to the German post-COVID/NAPKON trial landscape tracked on the trials side — a large negative RCT against the viral-persistence hypothesis.
