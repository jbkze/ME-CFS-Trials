# Orthostatic Dysfunction and Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome: A Close Reciprocal Relationship Beyond Cardiac Preload Failure and Hypoperfusion

## Summary

Standing up puts extra strain on the body, and people with ME/CFS often feel worse when upright (orthostatic intolerance). This paper argues that standing doesn't just strain the heart and blood vessels — it may also directly overload muscle cells with calcium, the same overload process Klaus Wirth has proposed causes post-exertional malaise (PEM, a crash after activity).

## Why this matters

Proposes that simply standing — not only physical exertion — could trigger PEM-like crashes in severely ill ME/CFS patients through one shared cellular mechanism, tying together two of the illness's most disabling symptoms; it's Wirth's own hypothesis, so high priority to watch for follow-up evidence.
