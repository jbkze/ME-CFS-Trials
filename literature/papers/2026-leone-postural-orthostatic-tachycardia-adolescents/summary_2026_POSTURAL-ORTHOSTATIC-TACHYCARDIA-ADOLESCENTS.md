## Summary

Doctors compared 18 teenagers with ME/CFS to 18 healthy teenagers using a simple 10-minute standing test that tracks heart rate and blood pressure. Over a third of the ME/CFS group turned out to have postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS, a condition where the heart races too much on standing up), while none of the healthy teens did — and just looking at how much the heart rate rose during that standing test, without needing an expert exam, identified it almost as well.

## Why this matters

Offers a simple, low-cost heart-rate-based test that non-specialist doctors could use to spot a treatable circulation problem (POTS) in teenagers with ME/CFS, potentially speeding up diagnosis and access to treatment.
