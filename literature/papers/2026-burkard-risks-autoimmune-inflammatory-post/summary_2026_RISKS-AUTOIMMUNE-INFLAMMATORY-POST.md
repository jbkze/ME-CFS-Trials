## Summary

This large study compared people who tested positive for COVID-19 with similar people who tested negative, tracking whether the COVID-positive group went on to develop more autoimmune and inflammatory conditions afterward — including ME/CFS. In the first nine months after infection, the study did not find a higher rate of ME/CFS diagnoses in the COVID-positive group compared to the COVID-negative group.

## Why this matters

A notable negative/null finding that pushes back against the idea that COVID-19 infection itself directly raises the short-term population-level risk of an ME/CFS diagnosis — worth weighing against other studies that do find a post-COVID ME/CFS signal, since it may reflect diagnostic-coding delays (ME/CFS is often only diagnosed well after 9 months) rather than a true absence of risk.

Published Jul 24, 2026. Large network/registry cohort study comparing COVID-test-positive vs test-negative individuals across multiple autoimmune/inflammatory outcomes, ME/CFS among them; first 9 months post-acute phase only. Not Wirth/Scheibenbogen-affiliated. Confirmed via BMJ publisher page.
