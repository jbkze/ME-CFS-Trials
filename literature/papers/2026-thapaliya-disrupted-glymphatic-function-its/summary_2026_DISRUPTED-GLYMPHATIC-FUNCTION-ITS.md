## Summary

Using a brain-scan technique that measures how well the brain clears waste while a person sleeps, researchers found that people with ME/CFS (31 patients vs 27 healthy people) have a noticeably weaker brain 'cleaning' system than healthy people, especially on the right side of the brain — and those with worse sleep and worse concentration/memory problems had the weakest waste-clearance.

_(Full text in `paper.txt`; the summary above is the curated plain-language abstract — deepen from the full text when reading.)_

## Why this matters

Points to a concrete physical mechanism — sluggish brain waste removal during sleep — that could help explain the brain fog and sleep disruption common in ME/CFS, and gives researchers a new measurable target for future biomarker or treatment studies.
