## Summary

A review paper explains how ancient virus fragments built into our own DNA long ago (human endogenous retroviruses, or HERVs) normally stay silent, but common infections like Epstein-Barr virus, HHV-6 or COVID-19 may switch some of them back on, and this reactivation could help drive the immune problems and brain inflammation seen in ME/CFS.

## Why this matters

Proposes a concrete biological trigger — reactivated 'fossil viruses' inside our own genome — that could explain why an infection can lead to long-term illness, and suggests measuring which of these HERVs are switched on could become a diagnostic test or way to track treatment response.
